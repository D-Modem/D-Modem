\section{Introduction}

D-Modem's modem core (\cmd{core/} in the source) takes the AT commands of
every D-Modem program:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{dmodemd}, the modem on a pseudo terminal
  (\cmd{/dev/ttyDM0} links to it), its line a SIP call;
\item \textbf{libdmodem} (\cmd{dmodem.h}), the modem as a library: the
  program that embeds it sends the commands;
\item the \textbf{Asterisk and FreeSWITCH modules} (\cmd{app\_dmodem},
  \cmd{mod\_dmodem}), whose profiles give the commands each call starts
  with.
\end{itemize}

The command set is D-Modem's own.  It began as Smart Link's slmodemd's
(the reference of Smart Link's ST56 modems is kept as
\cmd{docs/legacy/ST56ATCommands.pdf}) and has grown V.90 and V.92 as the
digital modem, V.91, V.44, V.92's modem-on-hold, and MNP 2 to 5 since.  It takes as
well the commands that other modems' setup strings send (V.250's,
Rockwell's and Conexant's, USRobotics'), most of them with no effect, so
that a setup string written for another modem works:
section~\ref{sec:compat} lists them, and where D-Modem's meaning of a
command differs from another modem's.  Section~\ref{sec:sip} tells what a
SIP line changes in dmodemd.

This reference follows the core as it is: every command, parameter range,
default and output here is the core's own.  A setting that is kept but
changes nothing says so.

\paragraph{Notation.} \cmd{AT} commands are shown in capitals; the modem
takes either case.  \p{n} is a decimal number, \p{h} a hexadecimal one;
\verb|[ ]| marks what may be left out.  A setting's default is the one
\cmdl[Z]{ATZ} and the start of the program give.

\section{Command lines}

\begin{itemize}
\item A command line begins with \cmd{AT} and ends with the character of
  \sreg{3} (carriage return) or \sreg{4} (line feed).  Blanks and tabs in it are
  dropped before it is read.
\item The character of \sreg{5} (backspace) takes back the last one typed.
\item A line holds 63 characters at most; a longer one is ERROR.
\item With echo on (\cmdl[E]{E1}), what the DTE types comes back as it is
  typed.
\item A line that does not begin with \cmd{AT} is not a command: with
  echo on it is blanked out on the DTE's screen.
\item \cmd{A/} (no line end needed) runs the last command line again.
\end{itemize}

The commands of a line run in turn, one after the other (\cmd{ATE1Q0V1});
a basic command's number left out is 0 (\cmd{ATE} is \cmd{ATE0}).  An
extended command (\cmd{+}\ldots) takes \cmd{=}\p{values}, \cmd{?} (its
values) and \cmd{=?} (its ranges).  A \cmd{;} may stand between two
commands, as V.250 puts one after an extended command that another
follows (\cmd{AT+IFC=2,2;+MS=V92}); here it is not needed.  The first
command that fails ends the line with ERROR; otherwise OK follows the
last one.  \cmd{A}, \cmd{D} and
\cmd{O} end the line and give their result later (CONNECT, NO CARRIER and
so on), and \cmd{Z} ends it with OK: whatever follows them on the line is
not read, but for a dial string's \cmd{;}, after which the rest of the
line runs once the number is dialed (\cmdl[D]{ATD}).  A command that reports something (\cmd{I}, \cmd{S}\p{n}\cmd{?},
\cmdl[plusMS-q]{+MS?} \ldots) prints it on lines of its own before the result.

A character from the DTE while a call is being set up (dialing,
answering, the modems' training) hangs up; after a dial string's
\cmd{;} the modem is in command state, and takes commands.

\section{Basic commands}

\begin{command}{A}{ATA}
Answers: off hook, and the answering modem's tones after \sreg{92} seconds.
In dmodemd the call that rings (\cmd{RING}) is answered.  Its result is
CONNECT, or NO CARRIER.  On a call held after a dial string's \cmd{;}
it answers that call (V.250's call-back: \cmd{ATD}\p{number}\cmd{;A}
dials, then answers).
\end{command}

\begin{command}{B}{ATB, ATB\p{n}}
\cmd{ATB} alone: V.22 at 1200 bit/s only, without automode,
\cmdl[plusMS]{AT+MS=22,0,1200,1200}.  \cmd{B0} to \cmd{B3}, \cmd{B15}
and \cmd{B16}, other modems' choice between the ITU and the Bell
modulation at 1200 and 300 bit/s: taken, changing nothing (the call's
negotiation and \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS} choose).
\end{command}

\begin{command}{D}{ATD\p{string}}
Dials \p{string} and calls.  The dialer sends the digits and
\cmd{* \# A B C D} as DTMF (\sreg{11} ms each, as long between them);
\cmd{T} and \cmd{P} choose tone or pulse dialing (\sreg{16}) for the
rest of the call, and these modifiers do as V.250 has them:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{>{\ttfamily}l p{0.75\textwidth}}
\toprule
, & a pause of \sreg{8} seconds \\
W & waits for a dial tone (1 s of it): NO DIALTONE if none comes in
  \sreg{7} seconds \\
@ & waits for the remote end's ringing (0.5 s of sound at least), then
  for 5 s of quiet after it: NO ANSWER if that does not come in
  \sreg{7} seconds \\
; & ends the dial string: once the number is dialed the modem is back
  in command state, off hook with the call kept, and says OK; the rest
  of the line runs then \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Other characters (\cmd{!}, the hook flash, among them: a SIP call has
no hook to flash) are skipped; \cmd{S=}\p{n} (a stored number) is ERROR,
as there are none.  \cmd{ATDL} dials the last number again
(\cmd{Dialing }\p{number}\cmd{...} first).  With \cmdl[X]{X2} or
\cmdl[X]{X4} the modem waits for a dial tone first.  The result is
CONNECT, BUSY, NO ANSWER, NO DIALTONE or NO CARRIER.

After a \cmd{;} the call waits, silent, for the DTE: \cmd{ATD}\p{more}
dials more on it (as DTMF; a \cmd{;} again holds it again),
\cmd{ATD} alone or \cmdl[O]{ATO} starts the calling modem's handshake,
\cmdl[A]{ATA} the answering modem's, and \cmdl[H]{ATH} hangs up (OK,
then NO CARRIER).  \cmd{ATDL} is ERROR then.  In dmodemd the dial
string is the SIP call's number (section~\ref{sec:sip}).
\end{command}

\begin{command}{E}{ATE[\p{n}]}
Echo of the DTE's characters in command mode: \cmd{E0} off, \cmd{E1} on
(\sreg{13}; default 1).
\end{command}

\begin{command}{H}{ATH[0], ATH1}
\cmd{H} or \cmd{H0} hangs up (on hook); \cmd{H1} goes off hook.
\end{command}

\begin{command}{I}{ATI[\p{n}]}
Information:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cl}
\toprule
\cmd{I0} & the product: \cmd{D-Modem} \\
\cmd{I1}, \cmd{I2} & the product and version (\cmd{D-Modem, 1.0.0}),
  then the maker, \cmd{D-Modem project} \\
\cmd{I3} & the modem's name, then its line's kind (dmodemd:
  \cmd{dmodem0}, \cmd{SIP}) \\
\cmd{I4} & \sreg{0} to S23, eight a line (\cmd{s00=000 s01=000 \ldots}) \\
\cmd{I5}, \cmd{I6} & \cmd{Stored Profile 0:}, \cmd{1:} (there are none) \\
\cmd{I7} & the country: \cmd{Country: USA} \\
\cmd{I11} & the last call's statistics, or this one's (below) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Any other number is ERROR.

\cmd{ATI11} lists the last call's statistics, as Conexant's modems do:
in online command state those of the call that is up, after a call
(dialed or answered, connected or not) its own, until the next one;
before any, \cmd{No Previous Call}.  A line whose value the call does
not have is left out (the rates and what follows for a call that never
came up, the frame counts without error correction, the SNR, the round
trip delay and the echoes where the modulation does not measure them,
the cause while the call is up):

-----------                         ------
Last Connection                     V34
Initial Transmit Carrier Rate       33600
...

A digital modem has no view of the line's digital loss or robbed-bit
signalling (the analogue modem measures them), so those other modems'
lines are not there.
\end{command}

\begin{command}{L}{ATL[\p{n}], ATM[\p{n}]}
The speaker's volume, \cmd{L0} to \cmd{L3} (\sreg{30}; default 3), and when it
is on, \cmd{M0} never, \cmd{M1} until the call is up, \cmd{M2} always,
\cmd{M3} as \cmd{M1} (on other modems not while dialing) (\sreg{29};
default 1).  A SIP line has no speaker: kept for the commands that set
them.
\end{command}

\begin{command}{N}{ATN[\p{n}]}
Automode (\sreg{31}; default 1): \cmd{N1} lets the modems find their best
common modulation from the one \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS} chose down, \cmd{N0} keeps to
it.  The same as \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS}'s second parameter.
\end{command}

\begin{command}{O}{ATO}
Back online after the escape (section~\ref{sec:online}): CONNECT.  On a
call held after a dial string's \cmd{;}, the calling modem's handshake
(as \cmdl[D]{ATD}).  ERROR when there is no call to go back to.
\end{command}

\begin{command}{P}{ATP, ATT}
Pulse or tone dialing (\sreg{16}; default tone).
\end{command}

\begin{command}{Q}{ATQ[\p{n}]}
\cmd{Q0} gives the result codes, \cmd{Q1} keeps them back (\sreg{14}; default
0).
\end{command}

\begin{command}{S}{ATS\p{n}?, ATS\p{n}=\p{v}}
Reads S-register \p{n} (0 to 255), in decimal on a line of its own, or
sets it to \p{v} (8 bits: \p{v} modulo 256).  A number over 255 is
ERROR.  Section~\ref{sec:sregs} lists them.
\end{command}

\begin{command}{V}{ATV[\p{n}]}
\cmd{V0} gives the result codes as numbers, \cmd{V1} as words (\sreg{15};
default 1).
\end{command}

\begin{command}{X}{ATX[\p{n}]}
Results and dial tone, \cmd{X0} to \cmd{X4} (\sreg{56}; default 4):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cll}
\toprule

While the line is taken (\cmd{+VLS=1}) the modem listens for DTMF
digits from the far end and reports them as V.253 has it: \cmd{<DLE>}
and the digit (0--9, *, \#, A--D) when one is heard, 51~ms into it, and
again every 70~ms while it lasts, then \cmd{<DLE>\textasciitilde} when
it ends.  The reports come in the voice command state as well as among
the samples of \cmd{+VRX}, whose audio is silence from a digit's report
until it ends.  The modem's own \cmd{+VTS} tones are not reported.

as \cmd{<DLE>r} (a tone of 400 to 480~Hz, or 440 and 480~Hz, or 400 and
450~Hz, on for 0.6~s at least; the UK's two bursts are one ring) and a
busy or congestion tone as \cmd{<DLE>b} (four rounds of 0.2 to 0.8~s on
and off), and an answer tone as \cmd{<DLE>a} (2100~Hz: a fax or data
answer) or \cmd{<DLE>f} (2225~Hz: a data answer), 0.3~s into it.  A ring
ends after 0.3~s without the tone.

reports in the transmit, receive and command states as hex bit fields
(bit \p{n} for V.253's event \p{n}, event 0 the leftmost of 32):
\begin{verbatim}
0,"",00000000,00000000,90000000
1,"T",08043060,08043060,08043060
\end{verbatim}
On hook (0) that is caller ID (with \cmdl[hashCID]{+VCID}) and RING; on the
line (1) DTMF, the loop current
interruption, a ringing tone, a busy tone and the two answer tones,
in every state.

When the far end ends a voice call, the modem reports \cmd{<DLE>l}
(V.253's loop current interruption, ``usually a remote hangup'') and
goes on hook (\cmd{+VLS=0}); V.253 has no NO CARRIER in voice mode.
What was under way ends: \cmd{+VRX}'s samples with \cmd{<DLE><ETX>},
then OK; \cmd{+VTX} and \cmd{+VTR} when the DTE ends them (its samples
until then are discarded), then OK, \cmd{+VTR}'s after
\cmd{<DLE><ETX>}; \cmd{+VTS}'s tones with ERROR.  In the command state
\cmd{<DLE>l} comes alone.

\section{S-registers}\label{sec:sregs}

\section{Online, the escape and hanging up}\label{sec:online}

Online, what the DTE sends goes to the other modem, except the escape:
three \sreg{2} characters (\cmd{+++}), each within 0.5 s of the one before,
with 0.5 s of nothing before them and after them.  The modem then says
OK and is in online command mode, the call still up: \cmdl[O]{ATO} goes back
online, \cmdl[H]{ATH} hangs up, and on a V.92 connection \cmdl[plusPMHR]{AT+PMHR} asks
for modem-on-hold.

The call ends with NO CARRIER when the other side hangs up or its
carrier is gone for \sreg{10} tenths of a second.  dmodemd hangs up as well
when the DTE drops DTR (it sets the pty's speed to 0) or, with HUPCL set
on the pty, closes it; it then makes a new pty (\cmd{/dev/ttyDM0} follows
it).

\section{dmodemd on a SIP line}\label{sec:sip}

\begin{itemize}
\item \cmdl[D]{ATD}\p{number} calls \cmd{sip:}\p{number}\cmd{@}\p{domain} of
  \cmd{SIP\_LOGIN}.  A telephone number (only V.250's dial characters:
  digits, \cmd{* \# + A B C D}, the modifiers \cmd{T P W , @ ! ;} and
  \cmd{- ( ) .} and blanks) goes as its digits and DTMF characters:
  \cmdl[D]{ATDT555-1000} calls \cmd{sip:5551000@}\p{domain}.  Anything else
  is a SIP user's name, as it is: \cmdl[D]{ATDalice}.
\item The line has no dial tone: \cmdl[X]{ATX3} (or \cmdl[X]{X1}), else
  NO DIALTONE.
\item A dial string with a \cmd{;} (\cmdl[D]{ATDT5551000;}) makes the
  SIP call and says OK at once, the modem in command state: what
  \cmd{ATD} adds then goes as DTMF on the call (a menu's choices, an
  extension), and \cmd{ATD} alone or \cmdl[A]{ATA} starts the modem's
  handshake.
\item With \cmd{-L} dmodemd registers and takes calls: RING every 3
  seconds while one waits, \cmdl[A]{ATA} or \sreg{0} rings (\cmdl[S]{ATS0=1}) answer
  it, and the rings stop if the caller gives up.  A call that comes while
  the line is in use is refused (486, busy); without \cmd{-L} every call
  is (480).
\item The call is G.711 at 8000 Hz, which is what V.90 and V.92 need
  (\cmdl[plusMS]{AT+MS=92}, the default).  Over a real PSTN gateway a lower
  modulation (\cmdl[plusMS]{AT+MS=132,0,4800,9600}) may do better.
\end{itemize}

\section{Setup strings from other modems}\label{sec:compat}

A setup string written for another modem (a program's, a driver's, a
guide's) holds commands of V.250, of Rockwell's and Conexant's modems,
of USRobotics' and of Hayes'.  The modem takes the common ones: those
it has a meaning for do what the sections above say, and the others,
below, are taken with no effect, so that the line ends in OK.  Flow
control is the pty's own and the line a G.711 call, and the
modulations retrain and renegotiate by themselves: what these commands
set elsewhere has no use here.

\subsection{Where D-Modem differs}

\begin{itemize}
\item \cmdl[ampK]{\&K} is the compression, as on USRobotics' modems;
  on Rockwell's and Conexant's it is flow control.  \cmd{\&K3} leaves
  V.42's compression on (MNP 5 off), \cmd{\&K0} turns it off.
\item The S-registers past \sreg{16} are D-Modem's own: \sreg{29} and
  \sreg{30} are the speaker's (elsewhere the hook flash time and the
  inactivity timer), \sreg{92} the answer's delay (elsewhere a fax
  transmit level: \cmd{ATS92=10} makes the modem wait 10 s before its
  answer tone).  The others (S36, S37, S48, S91 \ldots) are free: kept
  as written.  \sreg{7} and \sreg{11} start at 60 and 100.
\item The dial string's \cmd{!} (hook flash) and \cmd{\$} (bong tone)
  are skipped.  There are no stored numbers: \cmd{\&Z} and
  \cmd{ATDS=}\p{n} are ERROR.
\item \cmdl[I]{ATI}'s numbers are the modem's own (\cmd{I0} to
  \cmd{I7}, and \cmd{I11} as Conexant's); \cmd{ATI8} to \cmd{ATI10}
  and \cmd{ATI12} and up are ERROR.
\item The numeric results (\cmdl[V]{V0}) are Smart Link's, CONNECT's
  for each rate among them; those of USRobotics' and Conexant's modems
  differ.  CONNECT's rates without a number of Smart Link's have
  D-Modem's own, 80 to 87.
\item MNP is classes 2 to 5 (\cmdl[bsN]{\bs N}, \cmdl[ampK]{\%C}): there
  is no MNP 10 or 10EC.  A modem with MNP 10EC connects with V.42 (its
  ``EM'' detection patterns are passed over).  See
  \texttt{docs/link.md}, MNP.
\item K56flex is not there: \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS=56} runs V.90.
\end{itemize}
