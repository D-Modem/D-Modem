\section{Introduction}

D-Modem's modem core (\cmd{core/} in the source) takes the AT commands of
every D-Modem program:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{dmodemd}, the modem on a pseudo terminal
  (\cmd{/dev/ttyDM0} links to it), its line a SIP call;
\item \textbf{libdmodem} (\cmd{dmodem.h}), the modem as a library: the
  program that embeds it sends the commands;
\item the \textbf{Asterisk and FreeSWITCH modules} (\cmd{app\_dmodem},
  \cmd{mod\_dmodem}), whose profiles give the commands each call starts
  with.
\end{itemize}

The command set is D-Modem's own.  It began as Smart Link's slmodemd's
(the reference of Smart Link's ST56 modems is kept as
\cmd{docs/\allowbreak legacy/\allowbreak ST56ATCommands.pdf}) and has grown V.90 and V.92 as the
digital modem, V.91, V.44, V.92's modem-on-hold, and MNP 2 to 5 since.  It takes as
well the commands that other modems' setup strings send (V.250's,
Rockwell's and Conexant's, USRobotics'), most of them with no effect, so
that a setup string written for another modem works:
section~\ref{sec:compat} lists them, and where D-Modem's meaning of a
command differs from another modem's.  Section~\ref{sec:sip} tells what a
SIP line changes in dmodemd.

This reference follows the core as it is: every command, parameter range,
default and output here is the core's own.  A setting that is kept but
changes nothing says so.

\paragraph{Notation.} \cmd{AT} commands are shown in capitals; the modem
takes either case.  \p{n} is a decimal number, \p{h} a hexadecimal one;
\verb|[ ]| marks what may be left out.  A setting's default is the one
\cmdl[Z]{ATZ} and the start of the program give.

\section{Command lines}

\begin{itemize}
\item A command line begins with \cmd{AT} and ends with the character of
  \sreg{3} (carriage return) or \sreg{4} (line feed).  Blanks and tabs in it are
  dropped before it is read.
\item The character of \sreg{5} (backspace) takes back the last one typed.
\item A line holds 63 characters at most; a longer one is ERROR.
\item With echo on (\cmdl[E]{E1}), what the DTE types comes back as it is
  typed.
\item A line that does not begin with \cmd{AT} is not a command: with
  echo on it is blanked out on the DTE's screen.
\item \cmd{A/} (no line end needed) runs the last command line again.
\end{itemize}

The commands of a line run in turn, one after the other (\cmd{ATE1Q0V1});
a basic command's number left out is 0 (\cmd{ATE} is \cmd{ATE0}).  An
extended command (\cmd{+}\ldots) takes \cmd{=}\p{values}, \cmd{?} (its
values) and \cmd{=?} (its ranges).  A \cmd{;} may stand between two
commands, as V.250 puts one after an extended command that another
follows (\cmd{AT+IFC=2,2;+MS=V92}); here it is not needed.  The first
command that fails ends the line with ERROR; otherwise OK follows the
last one.  \cmd{A}, \cmd{D} and
\cmd{O} end the line and give their result later (CONNECT, NO CARRIER and
so on), and \cmd{Z} ends it with OK: whatever follows them on the line is
not read, but for a dial string's \cmd{;}, after which the rest of the
line runs once the number is dialed (\cmdl[D]{ATD}).  A command that reports something (\cmd{I}, \cmd{S}\p{n}\cmd{?},
\cmdl[plusMS-q]{+MS?} \ldots) prints it on lines of its own before the result.

A character from the DTE while a call is being set up (dialing,
answering, the modems' training) hangs up; after a dial string's
\cmd{;} the modem is in command state, and takes commands.

\section{Basic commands}

\begin{command}{A}{ATA}
Answers: off hook, and the answering modem's tones after \sreg{92} seconds.
In dmodemd the call that rings (\cmd{RING}) is answered.  Its result is
CONNECT, or NO CARRIER.  On a call held after a dial string's \cmd{;}
it answers that call (V.250's call-back: \cmd{ATD}\p{number}\cmd{;A}
dials, then answers).
\end{command}

\begin{command}{B}{ATB, ATB\p{n}}
\cmd{ATB} alone: V.22 at 1200 bit/s only, without automode,
\cmdl[plusMS]{AT+MS=22,0,1200,1200}.  \cmd{B0} to \cmd{B3}, \cmd{B15}
and \cmd{B16}, other modems' choice between the ITU and the Bell
modulation at 1200 and 300 bit/s: taken, changing nothing (the call's
negotiation and \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS} choose).
\end{command}

\begin{command}{D}{ATD\p{string}}
Dials \p{string} and calls.  The dialer sends the digits and
\cmd{* \# A B C D} as DTMF (\sreg{11} ms each, as long between them);
\cmd{T} and \cmd{P} choose tone or pulse dialing (\sreg{16}) for the
rest of the call.  Pulses are dialed on an analogue line (dmodemd's
\texttt{-a} and \texttt{--usb}), with the country's break, make, gap
between digits and digit pattern (\cmdl[plusGCI]{+GCI}; \cmd{* \# A B C
D} skipped); a line that cannot pulse, such as SIP, dials them in DTMF.
These modifiers do as V.250 has them:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{>{\ttfamily}l p{0.75\textwidth}}
\toprule
, & a pause of \sreg{8} seconds \\
W & waits for a dial tone (1 s of it): NO DIALTONE if none comes in
  \sreg{7} seconds \\
@ & waits for the remote end's ringing (0.5 s of sound at least), then
  for 5 s of quiet after it: NO ANSWER if that does not come in
  \sreg{7} seconds \\
; & ends the dial string: once the number is dialed the modem is back
  in command state, off hook with the call kept, and says OK; the rest
  of the line runs then \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Other characters (\cmd{!}, the hook flash, among them: a SIP call has
no hook to flash) are skipped; \cmd{S=}\p{n} (a stored number) is ERROR,
as there are none.  \cmd{ATDL} dials the last number again
(\cmd{Dialing }\p{number}\cmd{...} first).  With \cmdl[X]{X2} or
\cmdl[X]{X4} the modem waits for a dial tone first.  The result is
CONNECT, BUSY, NO ANSWER, NO DIALTONE or NO CARRIER.

After a \cmd{;} the call waits, silent, for the DTE: \cmd{ATD}\p{more}
dials more on it (as DTMF; a \cmd{;} again holds it again),
\cmd{ATD} alone or \cmdl[O]{ATO} starts the calling modem's handshake,
\cmdl[A]{ATA} the answering modem's, and \cmdl[H]{ATH} hangs up (OK,
then NO CARRIER).  \cmd{ATDL} is ERROR then.  In dmodemd the dial
string is the SIP call's number (section~\ref{sec:sip}).
\end{command}

\begin{command}{E}{ATE[\p{n}]}
Echo of the DTE's characters in command mode: \cmd{E0} off, \cmd{E1} on
(\sreg{13}; default 1).
\end{command}

\begin{command}{H}{ATH[0], ATH1}
\cmd{H} or \cmd{H0} hangs up (on hook); \cmd{H1} goes off hook.
\end{command}

\begin{command}{I}{ATI[\p{n}]}
Information:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.72\textwidth}}
\toprule
\cmd{I0} & the product: \cmd{D-Modem} \\

V.29, V.33 and V.27ter are the 4-wire leased line modulations
(\cmdl[ampL]{\&L2}): each end sends on its own pair, on a carrier that
stays on.  \p{max} sets the rate each end sends at, \p{min} the lowest
it comes down to: a receiver finds the far end's rate by itself, an end
faster than the far end comes down to its rate, and one that loses its
equalization twice in 20~s at its own rate takes the next rate down.
A receiver that loses its equalization sends its synchronizing signal
again and gets the far end's back (V.29 10, V.33 9).

V.90 and V.92 are there as the \emph{digital} modem (the server's side,
as on a G.711 VoIP line), on an 8000 Hz G.711 line: analogue V.90 and
V.92 modems calling connect at up to 56000 bit/s downstream.  90 and 92
offer V.91 too, which two digital modems use instead, and V.34 for the
others; on a line at another sample rate they are V.34.
\label{sec:analogue}On an analogue telephone line (dmodemd's \texttt{-a}
and \texttt{--usb}) the modem is the call's analogue end instead: V.90
and V.92 run as the analogue modem against a V.90 or V.92 server (at
9600~Hz; see \texttt{docs/hardware.md}), and V.91 and \cmd{CLEAR}, which
need a digital line, run as V.34.  With automode
V.8 and the V.34 modem hand over to V.32bis (and its V.22bis and V.22)
for a modem without V.34, and the answering modem tries V.23 and Bell
103 for one without V.8.

\cmd{V\_CVM} is the proposed Recommendation of \cmd{docs/v\_cvm.pdf}, for
calls whose speech path is a mobile codec's (AMR, AMR-WB), which carry
neither V.34 nor V.32: V.8 with V.22 and V.21 as the fallback, then a
probing of the codec and a mode loaded for it.  The start-up takes 15 to
21~s; \sreg{7} should allow for it.  The connection runs LAPM.

\cmd{VWB} is D-Modem's own mode for a bare copper pair between two of
its modems (pico-serial's WB stick, a codec at 96 kHz): discrete
multitone, the caller on the lower tones and the answerer on the upper,
each tone loaded from its measured SNR; see \cmd{docs/dsp/vwb.md}.  It
runs at 48000 Hz and more only, where it is the only mode and the
default (\cmd{ATZ} sets it again there), as a leased line
(\cmdl[ampL]{\&L1}); the rates of \cmd{+MS} do not limit it.

\cmd{CLEAR} is a clear 64 kbit/s digital channel, such as an ISDN
B-channel's data call (a SIP call in CLEARMODE, RFC 4040, or a PBX's call
with an unrestricted digital bearer): no modulation, each of the
channel's octets 8 bits of the link's, \cmd{CONNECT 64000} both ways.
It starts at once on \cmd{ATD} and \cmd{ATA}, with no call progress
and no answer tone, with a start-up of D-Modem's own (HDLC flags each
way, then ones), so the far end is another D-Modem; V.42 and the
compressions run over it as over any modulation.  A line that says its
call is clear (dmodemd's SIP for a CLEARMODE call, \cmd{app\_dmodem}
for an ISDN data call) runs it whatever \cmd{+MS} says.  What runs
on the channel is \cmdl[plusB2]{+B2}'s, X.75 by default.  See
\cmd{docs/isdn.md}.

\emph{Pending:} ISDN's second phase, X.75 and synchronous PPP on the
clear channel and the command that chooses them (\cmd{AT+B2}, with
CAPI's layer 2 numbers), is being added; this edition does not
describe it yet.
\end{command}

\subsection{V.80 Synchronous Access Mode: +ESA, +ITF}
\label{sec:v80}

With \cmdl[plusES]{+ES=6} (calling) or \cmd{+ES=,,8} (answering) the
call has no error correction or compression: after CONNECT the modem
moves the DTE's octets as a synchronous bit stream, with V.80's in-band
commands after EM (19h) both ways (Table~9/V.80).  It starts in the
transparent sub-mode: the DTE's octets go out as they are, least
significant bit first, and the line's bits come back as octets; when the
DTE has nothing the modem sends \p{syn1} and tells it
\cmd{<EM><under>}.  With \cmdl[plusESA]{+ESA}'s \p{trans\_idle} 1 or 2
the receiver first hunts for the SYN, and gives the DTE nothing before
it.  \cmd{<EM><flag>} from the DTE starts the framed
sub-mode: HDLC frames, each ended by the DTE's \cmd{<EM><flag>}, the
modem adding the zero insertion, the flags and the FCS; it gives back
each received frame's octets (without the FCS it checked) and
\cmd{<EM><flag>} for a good one or \cmd{<EM><err>} for a bad one, an
\cmd{<EM><err>} when its hunt finds a flag, and \cmd{<EM><mark>} for an
abort or marks.  \cmd{<EM><mark>} from the DTE goes back to transparent.
EM and 99h in the data are shielded both ways (\cmd{<EM><t1>},
\cmd{<EM><t2>}; DC1 and DC3 too with DC1/DC3 flow control,
\cmd{+IFC}); from the DTE all the codes T1 to T20 are taken.

The other commands: \cmd{<EM><ecs>} (instead of \cmd{+++}, which is
data) gives \cmd{<EM><ecs>}, then OK in online command state, where
\cmd{ATO} goes back; \cmd{<EM><eot>} gives \cmd{<EM><eot>} and hangs up
(NO CARRIER); \cmd{<EM><rate><tx><rx>} sets the highest rates to send at (\cmd{+MS}'s
\p{max}) from \p{tx} and to receive at from \p{rx}, for the next
retrain or renegotiation (\cmd{AT+MS} sets both again); \cmd{<EM><hunt>} restarts the receiver's hunt (framed, or
transparent with \p{trans\_idle} 1 or 2), what waits for the DTE dropped;
\cmd{<EM><resume>} after an underrun (below).  \cmd{<EM><rtn>}
retrains the call and \cmd{<EM><rrn>} renegotiates its rate, as
\cmdl[O]{ATO1} and \cmd{ATO3}.  The modem gives
\cmd{<EM><eot>} before NO CARRIER when the remote's carrier goes,
\cmd{<EM><rtn>} and \cmd{<EM><rrn>} for each retrain and rate
renegotiation but the DTE's own (the remote's, or the modem's after a
loss of signal), \cmd{<EM><rate><tx><rx>} after each, or when the rates
change in the call,
\cmd{<EM><rover>} where received octets were lost (the DTE took nothing
for 2048 octets), \cmd{<EM><tover>} where its own were (the buffer
full), and \cmd{<EM><bnum>} with \cmd{+ITF}.  V.34's half duplex is
not there.

\begin{command}{plusESA}{AT+ESA=[\p{trans\_idle}][,\p{framed\_idle}...][,\p{syn2}], AT+ESA?, AT+ESA=?}
The Synchronous Access Mode's eight settings, each left out as it is (default
\cmd{0,0,0,0,0,0,255,255}): \p{trans\_idle}, the transparent
sub-mode's SYN, 0 \p{syn1} sent when the DTE has nothing and the
receiver's octets as they come, 1 the same but the receiver hunting for
\p{syn1}, 2 \p{syn1} and \p{syn2} sent in turn and the receiver hunting
for the two (\p{syn1} first), each hunt giving the DTE the octets from
the SYN on, aligned on it; \p{framed\_idle}
between frames 0 flags, 1 marks; \p{framed\_un\_ov} when the DTE runs out
in a frame, 0 an abort, 1 a flag (no FCS), the DTE's octets then dropped
until its \cmd{<EM><resume>}, which \cmd{<EM><unum>} and their count
answer (both: \cmd{<EM><under>}); \p{hd\_auto} 0; \p{crc\_type} 0 no FCS,
1 FCS-16, 2 FCS-32 (V.42's); \p{nrzi\_en} 0; \p{syn1} and \p{syn2}
as above.
\cmd{=?} gives \cmd{(0-2),(0,1),(0,1),(0),(0-2),(0),(0-255),(0-255)}.
\end{command}

\begin{command}{plusITF}{AT+ITF=[\p{off}][,[\p{on}][,[\p{report\_period}]]], AT+ITF?, AT+ITF=?}
The Synchronous Access Mode's transmit buffer (default \cmd{383,128,0}):
the DTE's octets in it, EM codes included, stop being taken above
\p{off} (\p{on} is kept; \p{off} must be above it, else ERROR), and with
\p{report\_period} not 0 the modem gives \cmd{<EM><bnum>} with their
number every \p{report\_period} times 10~ms.  \cmd{=?} gives
\cmd{(1-4095),(0-4094),(0-500)}.
\end{command}

Each \cmd{+} command takes \cmd{?} (its values) and \cmd{=?} (their ranges).
\cmd{+VCID}, caller ID, is \cmdl[hashCID]{\#CID}'s, in every mode.

While the line is taken (\cmd{+VLS=1}) the modem listens for DTMF
digits from the far end and reports them as V.253 has it: \cmd{<DLE>}
and the digit (0--9, *, \#, A--D) when one is heard, 51~ms into it, and
again every 70~ms while it lasts, then \cmd{<DLE>\textasciitilde} when
it ends.  The reports come in the voice command state as well as among
the samples of \cmd{+VRX}, whose audio is silence from a digit's report
until it ends.  The modem's own \cmd{+VTS} tones are not reported.

The modem also reports, in every state, each ring of a ringing tone
as \cmd{<DLE>r} (a tone of 400 to 480~Hz, or 440 and 480~Hz, or 400 and
450~Hz, on for 0.6~s at least; the UK's two bursts are one ring) and a
busy or congestion tone as \cmd{<DLE>b} (four rounds of 0.2 to 0.8~s on
and off), and an answer tone as \cmd{<DLE>a} (2100~Hz: a fax or data
answer) or \cmd{<DLE>f} (2225~Hz: a data answer), 0.3~s into it.  A ring
ends after 0.3~s without the tone.  A calling fax machine's CNG
(1100~Hz, bursts of 0.36 to 0.8~s) is \cmd{<DLE>c}, a calling data
modem's V.25 tone (1300~Hz) \cmd{<DLE>e}, each as a burst ends and 4~s
apart at least, as V.253 asks.

\cmd{AT+VLS=?} gives V.253's list of the devices, with the events each
reports in the transmit, receive and command states as hex bit fields
(bit \p{n} for V.253's event \p{n}, event 0 the leftmost of 32):
\begin{verbatim}
0,"",00000000,00000000,90000000
1,"T",0B043060,0B043060,0B043060
\end{verbatim}
On hook (0) that is caller ID (with \cmdl[hashCID]{+VCID}) and RING; on the
line (1) DTMF, the two calling tones, the loop current interruption, a
ringing tone, a busy tone and the two answer tones, in every state.  A
line that senses the phone on the modem's own jack (a SmartUSB56 with
\texttt{dmodemd --usb-handset}) adds event 8 to both, in every state
(\texttt{00800000}, \texttt{0B843060}, \ldots): that phone off hook is
\cmd{<DLE>H}, on hook again \cmd{<DLE>h}.  A modem with a handset of its own
(a SmartUSB56 with \texttt{dmodemd --usb-voice-codec}) lists two more:
\begin{verbatim}
14,"H",08000000,08000000,08000000
15,"HT",0B043060,0B043060,0B043060
\end{verbatim}
\cmd{+VLS=14} puts the voice engine on the handset alone, the line on
hook: \cmd{+VRX} records its microphone, \cmd{+VTX} plays to its
earpiece, and DTMF from it is reported.  \cmd{+VLS=15} takes the line
with the handset on the call: the DTE hears the line and the handset,
the line gets the DTE's samples and the handset.  Moving between 1, 14
and 15 keeps the voice engine running, the line taken or left.

A voice call can become a fax or data call (V.253 5.3.2 and its
example I.6).  \cmdl[plusFCLASS]{+FCLASS=1} (or \cmd{0}) in the voice
command state, off hook, keeps the call: the voice engine stops, the
line stays off hook and silent, and \cmdl[A]{A} answers on it as a fax
(class 1: CED, then CONNECT for the DIS) or data modem, or
\cmdl[D]{D} calls on it (call progress waits for the far end's answer
tone).  \cmdl[H]{H}, \cmdl[Z]{Z} or the far end hanging up ends the held
call.  For example, a fax machine calls while the greeting plays:
\begin{verbatim}
AT+VTX                 CONNECT      the greeting's samples follow
                       <DLE>c       the caller's CNG
<DLE><ETX>             OK
AT+FCLASS=1            OK
ATA                    CONNECT      class 1, as after AT+FTH=3
\end{verbatim}
With \cmdl[plusFAA]{+FAA=1} the modem does it itself, in the voice
command state (a \cmd{+VTX}, \cmd{+VRX}, \cmd{+VTR} or \cmd{+VTS} under
way ends first): after \cmd{<DLE>c}, FAX, \cmd{+FCLASS=1} and the fax
answer's CONNECT; after \cmd{<DLE>e}, \cmd{+FCLASS=0} and the adaptive
data answer (DATA, then its CONNECT).  \cmd{+VNH} is accepted and has no
effect: a held call's failed handshake hangs up.

When the far end ends a voice call, the modem reports \cmd{<DLE>l}
(V.253's loop current interruption, ``usually a remote hangup'') and
goes on hook (\cmd{+VLS=0}); V.253 has no NO CARRIER in voice mode.
What was under way ends: \cmd{+VRX}'s samples with \cmd{<DLE><ETX>},
then OK; \cmd{+VTX} and \cmd{+VTR} when the DTE ends them (its samples
until then are discarded), then OK, \cmd{+VTR}'s after
\cmd{<DLE><ETX>}; \cmd{+VTS}'s tones with ERROR.  In the command state
\cmd{<DLE>l} comes alone.

\section{Online, the escape and hanging up}\label{sec:online}

Online, what the DTE sends goes to the other modem, except the escape:
three \sreg{2} characters (\cmd{+++}), each within 0.5 s of the one before,
with 0.5 s of nothing before them and after them.  The modem then says
OK and is in online command mode, the call still up: \cmdl[O]{ATO} goes back
online, \cmdl[H]{ATH} hangs up, and on a V.92 connection \cmdl[plusPMHR]{AT+PMHR} asks
for modem-on-hold.  The \cmd{+++} itself is data (the modem cannot know
it is the escape until the guard time after it), and what the DTE sent
before the escape and the line had not taken yet is kept, not dropped:
it goes to the other modem, \cmd{+++} last, once \cmd{ATO} is back
online.

The call ends with NO CARRIER when the other side hangs up or its
carrier is gone for \sreg{10} tenths of a second.  dmodemd hangs up as well
when the DTE drops DTR (it sets the pty's speed to 0) or, with HUPCL set
on the pty, closes it; it then makes a new pty (\cmd{/dev/ttyDM0} follows
it).

\section{dmodemd on a SIP line}\label{sec:sip}

\begin{itemize}
\item \cmdl[D]{ATD}\p{number} calls \cmd{sip:}\p{number}\cmd{@}\p{domain} of
  \cmd{SIP\_LOGIN}.  A telephone number (only V.250's dial characters:
  digits, \cmd{* \# + A B C D}, the modifiers \cmd{T P W , @ ! ;} and
  \cmd{- ( ) .} and blanks) goes as its digits and DTMF characters:
  \cmdl[D]{ATDT555-1000} calls \cmd{sip:5551000@}\p{domain}.  Anything else
  is a SIP user's name, as it is: \cmdl[D]{ATDalice}.
\item The line has no dial tone: \cmdl[X]{ATX3} (or \cmdl[X]{X1}), else
  NO DIALTONE.
\item A dial string with a \cmd{;} (\cmdl[D]{ATDT5551000;}) makes the
  SIP call and says OK at once, the modem in command state: what
  \cmd{ATD} adds then goes as DTMF on the call (a menu's choices, an
  extension), and \cmd{ATD} alone or \cmdl[A]{ATA} starts the modem's
  handshake.
\item With \cmd{-L} dmodemd registers and takes calls: RING every 3
  seconds while one waits, \cmdl[A]{ATA} or \sreg{0} rings (\cmdl[S]{ATS0=1}) answer
  it, and the rings stop if the caller gives up.  A call that comes while
  the line is in use is refused (486, busy); without \cmd{-L} every call
  is (480).
\item The call is G.711 at 8000 Hz, which is what V.90 and V.92 need
  (\cmdl[plusMS]{AT+MS=92}, the default).  Over a real PSTN gateway a lower
  modulation (\cmdl[plusMS]{AT+MS=132,0,4800,9600}) may do better.
\end{itemize}

\section{Setup strings from other modems}\label{sec:compat}

A setup string written for another modem (a program's, a driver's, a
guide's) holds commands of V.250, of Rockwell's and Conexant's modems,
of USRobotics' and of Hayes'.  The modem takes the common ones: those
it has a meaning for do what the sections above say, and the others,
below, are taken with no effect, so that the line ends in OK.  Flow
control is the pty's own and the line a G.711 call, and the
modulations retrain and renegotiate by themselves: what these commands
set elsewhere has no use here.

\subsection{Where D-Modem differs}

\begin{itemize}
\item \cmdl[ampK]{\&K} is the compression, as on USRobotics' modems;
  on Rockwell's and Conexant's it is flow control.  \cmd{\&K3} leaves
  V.42's compression on (MNP 5 off), \cmd{\&K0} turns it off.
\item The S-registers past \sreg{16} are D-Modem's own: \sreg{29} and
  \sreg{30} are the speaker's (elsewhere the hook flash time and the
  inactivity timer), \sreg{92} the answer's delay (elsewhere a fax
  transmit level: \cmd{ATS92=10} makes the modem wait 10 s before its
  answer tone).  The others (S36, S37, S91 \ldots) are free: kept
  as written.  \sreg{7} and \sreg{11} start at 60 and 100.
\item The dial string's \cmd{!} (hook flash) and \cmd{\$} (bong tone)
  are skipped.  There are no stored numbers: \cmd{\&Z} and
  \cmd{ATDS=}\p{n} are ERROR.
\item \cmdl[I]{ATI}'s numbers are the modem's own (\cmd{I0} to
  \cmd{I7}), but for the call statistics: \cmd{I6} as USRobotics',
  \cmd{I11} as Conexant's, \cmdl[ampV]{\&V1} as Rockwell's; \cmd{ATI8}
  to \cmd{ATI10} and \cmd{ATI12} and up are ERROR.
\item The numeric results (\cmdl[V]{V0}) are Smart Link's, CONNECT's
  for each rate among them; those of USRobotics' and Conexant's modems
  differ.  CONNECT's rates without a number of Smart Link's have
  D-Modem's own, 80 to 87.
\item MNP is classes 2 to 5 (\cmdl[bsN]{\bs N}, \cmdl[ampK]{\%C}): there
  is no MNP 10 or 10EC.  A modem with MNP 10EC connects with V.42 (its
  ``EM'' detection patterns are passed over).  See
  \texttt{docs/link.md}, MNP.
\item K56flex is not there: \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS=56} runs V.90.
\end{itemize}
