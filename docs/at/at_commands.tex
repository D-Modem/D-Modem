\section{Introduction}

This manual specifies the AT command set of D-Modem's modem core
(\cmd{core/} in the source).  The core takes the commands of every
D-Modem program:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{dmodemd}, the modem on a pseudo terminal
  (\cmd{/dev/ttyDM0} links to it), its line a SIP call or, with
  \texttt{-a} and \texttt{--usb}, an analogue telephone line;
\item \textbf{libdmodem} (\cmd{dmodem.h}), the modem as a library: the
  program that embeds it sends the commands;
\item the \textbf{Asterisk and FreeSWITCH modules} (\cmd{app\_dmodem},
  \cmd{mod\_dmodem}), whose profiles give the commands each call starts
  with.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{What the modem does}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Data} from 300~bit/s to 56000~bit/s: \std{V.21}, \std{V.22},
  \std{V.22bis}, \std{V.23}, Bell 103 and 212A, \std{V.32}, \std{V.32bis},
  \std{V.34} up to 33600~bit/s, and \std{V.90} and \std{V.92} as the
  digital modem.  Automode finds the best modulation both ends have
  (\hyperref[sec:modulation]{Section~\ref*{sec:modulation}}).
\item \textbf{Error correction and compression}: \std{V.42} (LAPM) and
  \std{MNP} 2 to 5; \std{V.42bis}, \std{V.44}, \std{MNP~5} and zstd
  (\hyperref[sec:errctl]{Sections~\ref*{sec:errctl}} and
  \hyperref[sec:compression]{\ref*{sec:compression}}).
\item \textbf{V.92's extras}: quick connect, PCM upstream and
  modem-on-hold, to take a waiting call and come back to the data call.
\item \textbf{Fax}: \std{Class 1} (\std{T.31}) and \std{Class 2} and \std{Class 2.0} (\std{T.32}),
  up to 14400~bit/s with \std{V.17}, and adaptive answer, which tells a fax
  call from a data call (\hyperref[sec:fax]{Section~\ref*{sec:fax}}).
\item \textbf{Voice} (\std{V.253}): play and record audio, send and
  detect DTMF and tones, use a handset, and turn a voice call into a fax
  or data call (\hyperref[sec:voice]{Section~\ref*{sec:voice}}).
\item \textbf{Text telephones} (\std{V.18}): the 5-bit TTY of the deaf
  community, telex and RTTY, EDT, and automoding between them
  (\hyperref[sec:text]{Section~\ref*{sec:text}}).
\item \textbf{Caller ID}, from a SIP call or a telephone line (FSK and
  DTMF), and a waiting call's caller in voice mode.
\item \textbf{Leased lines}, 2-wire and 4-wire (\std{V.29}, \std{V.33},
  \std{V.27ter}), with \std{V.13}'s simulated carrier control.
\item \textbf{Digital channels}: \std{V.91} between two digital modems,
  the clear 64000~bit/s channel of an ISDN data call (\std{X.75}, PPP,
  \std{V.110}, \std{V.120}) (\hyperref[sec:isdn]{Section~\ref*{sec:isdn}}),
  and \std{V.80}'s synchronous access.
\item \textbf{V.cvm} for data over mobile speech paths, and
  \textbf{V.wb}, a wideband modem for a bare pair of wires.
\item \textbf{Diagnostics}: the last call's statistics (\cmdl[I]{ATI11},
  \cmdl[ampV]{AT\&V1}, \cmdl[hashUD]{AT\#UD}) and loop tests
  (\cmdl[ampT]{AT\&T}).
\item \textbf{Other modems' setup strings} work: the modem accepts the
  commands of \std{V.250}, Rockwell and Conexant, and USRobotics modems,
  most of them with no effect; their entries say so.
\end{itemize}

Every command, range, default and response in this manual is the
core's own.  A setting that is kept but changes nothing says so.

\paragraph{How to read this manual.}  Each command has an entry of the
same form: its name and title in a strip, what it does, then where it
has them its syntax, its result codes and a numbered table of its
values, the default marked \emph{(default)}.  \hyperref[tab:summary]{Table~\ref*{tab:summary}}
lists every command and links to its entry.  Commands are shown in
capitals; the modem takes either case.  \p{n} is a decimal number,
\p{h} a hexadecimal one; \verb|[ ]| marks what may be left out.  A
setting's default is the one \cmdl[Z]{ATZ} and the start of the program
give.

\section{Common terms}\label{sec:terms}

A few words this manual uses throughout.  The glossary
(\hyperref[sec:glossary]{Section~\ref*{sec:glossary}}) explains the
rarer ones.

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.2em,font=\bfseries\color{dmnavy}]
\item[AT command] AT stands for ``attention''.  An instruction to the
  modem, sent as text that starts with the letters \cmdr{AT}, such as \cmdl[Z]{ATZ}.  One
  line can hold several commands (\cmdr{ATE1Q0V1}).
\item[DTE] Data Terminal Equipment.  The computer or program that talks
  to the modem: a terminal program, a fax program, \texttt{pppd},
  or the program that embeds libdmodem.
\item[DCE] Data Circuit-terminating Equipment.  The modem: what sits
  between the DTE and the telephone line or call, here D-Modem itself.
\item[Command mode and online mode] In command mode the modem reads what
  the DTE sends as commands.  Once a call is up (\rc{CONNECT}) it is
  online: what the DTE sends goes to the far end as data.  The escape
  sequence (\cmdr{+++}, with a pause before and after) returns to
  command mode with the call kept, and \cmdl[O]{ATO} goes back online
  (\hyperref[sec:online]{Section~\ref*{sec:online}}).
\item[Result code] The modem's answer to a command line, or news of a
  call: \rc{OK}, \rc{ERROR}, \rc{CONNECT}, \rc{RING}, \rc{NO CARRIER}
  and so on (\hyperref[sec:results]{Section~\ref*{sec:results}}).
\item[S-register] A numbered setting, read with \cmdr{ATS}\p{n}\cmdr{?}
  and set with \cmdr{ATS}\p{n}\cmdr{=}\p{value}
  (\hyperref[sec:sregs]{Section~\ref*{sec:sregs}}).
\item[Basic and extended commands] Basic commands are a letter, maybe
  after \cmdr{\&}, \cmdr{\bs}, or \cmdr{\%}, and a number
  (\cmdl[E]{E1}, \cmdl[ampK]{\&K3}).  Extended commands start with
  \cmdr{+} or \cmdr{-} and take values (\cmdl[plusMS]{+MS=V92}).
\item[Default] A setting's value after \cmdl[Z]{ATZ} or when the program
  starts.  The modem keeps no stored profiles.
\item[Modulation] How the modem turns data into sound on the line, and
  so how fast it goes: \std{V.34}, \std{V.90}, \std{V.92} and the rest,
  chosen with \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS}.
\item[Carrier] The signal the far modem sends during a call.  Losing it
  ends the call (\rc{NO CARRIER}).
\item[bit/s] Bits per second, the speed of a connection; \rc{CONNECT}
  gives it.
\item[Error correction and compression] Error correction (\std{V.42},
  \std{MNP}) resends data the line damaged; compression (\std{V.42bis},
  \std{V.44}) packs it to go faster.  Both need the far modem to have
  them too.
\item[SIP] Session Initiation Protocol.  The protocol of internet
  telephony.  dmodemd places and takes
  its calls as SIP calls (\hyperref[sec:sip]{Section~\ref*{sec:sip}}).
\item[Dial string] What follows \cmdl[D]{ATD}: the number and the
  modifiers that change how it is dialed (\cmdl[D]{T}, \cmdl[D]{P},
  \cmdl[D]{W}, \cmdl[D]{,} and so on).
\end{description}

\section{Command lines}

AT commands are issued to the modem to control its operation and its
settings.  The modem takes them in command mode: after it starts, when
a call ends, after any command but a dial or an answer, and online
after the escape sequence (\hyperref[sec:online]{Section~\ref*{sec:online}}), the call still
up.  Each command line is answered with a result code
(\hyperref[sec:results]{Section~\ref*{sec:results}}).

\begin{itemize}
\item A command line begins with \cmdr{AT} and ends with the character of
  \sreg{3} (carriage return) or \sreg{4} (line feed).  Blanks and tabs
  in it are dropped before it is read.
\item The character of \sreg{5} (backspace) takes back the last one
  typed.
\item A line holds 63 characters at most; a longer one is \rc{ERROR}.
\item With echo on (\cmdl[E]{E1}), what the DTE types comes back as it
  is typed.
\item A line that does not begin with \cmdr{AT} is not a command: with
  echo on it is blanked out on the DTE's screen.
\end{itemize}

The commands of a line run in turn (\cmdr{ATE1Q0V1}).  A basic command's
number left out is 0 (\cmdl[E]{ATE} is \cmdl[E]{ATE0}).  An extended command
(\cmdr{+}\ldots) takes \cmdr{=}\p{values}, \cmdr{?} (its values) and
\cmdr{=?} (its ranges).  A \cmdr{;} may stand between two commands, as
\std{V.250} puts one after an extended command that another follows
(\cmdr{AT+IFC=2,2;+MS=V92}); here it is not needed.  A value left out of
an extended command's \cmdr{=}\p{values} keeps what it was.

Some extended parameters (\cmdl[plusILRR]{+ILRR}, \cmdl[plusMR]{+MR},
\cmdl[plusPCW]{+PCW}, \cmdl[minusSEC]{-SEC} and others) are kept for
other modems' setup strings, with no other effect but where an entry
says so.  \cmdr{=}\p{values} sets them (each in its range, else \rc{ERROR}),
\cmdr{?} gives \rc{+\p{name}: \p{values}}, \cmdr{=?} the ranges,
and \cmdl[Z]{ATZ} the defaults.

The commands are grouped by what they do, each group a section: echo
and result codes, settings and profiles, identification, dialling and
answering, the speaker and the line, modulation, error control, data
compression, the DTE interface, ISDN, then fax, text telephones and
voice.  \hyperref[tab:summary]{Table~\ref*{tab:summary}} lists them by name.

The first command that fails ends the line with \rc{ERROR}; otherwise \rc{OK}
follows the last one.  \cmdl[A]{A}, \cmdl[D]{D} and \cmdl[O]{O} end the line and
give their result later (\rc{CONNECT}, \rc{NO CARRIER} and so on), and \cmdl[Z]{Z}
ends it with \rc{OK}: whatever follows them on the line is not read, but
for a dial string's \cmdl[D]{;}, after which the rest of the line runs once
the number is dialed (\cmdl[D]{ATD}).  A command that reports something
(\cmdl[I]{I}, \cmdl[S]{S}\p{n}\cmdr{?}, \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS?} \ldots) prints it on
lines of its own before the result.

A character from the DTE while a call is being set up (dialing,
answering, the modems' training) hangs up.  After a dial string's
\cmdl[D]{;} the modem is in command state, and takes commands.

\begin{command}{S}{S\p{n}}{Read or Set an S-Register}
\cmdl[S]{ATS}\p{n}\cmd{?} reads S-register \p{n} (0 to 255) and gives its
value in decimal on a line of its own.  \cmdl[S]{ATS}\p{n}\cmd{=}\p{v}
sets it to \p{v}; the registers hold 8 bits, so \p{v} is taken modulo
256.  Both numbers are decimal, a leading 0 too (\cmdl[S]{ATS09?} is S9).
\cmdl[S]{ATS103=}\p{n} is \cmdl[bsN]{\bs N}\p{n}.  \hyperref[sec:sregs]{Section~\ref*{sec:sregs}}

\begin{syntax}
\item ATS\p{n}?
\item ATS\p{n}=\p{v}
\end{syntax}
\begin{results}
\res{\rc{OK}}{Always.}
\res{\rc{ERROR}}{For a number over 255, a read-only register (\sreg{44}, \sreg{45}), or neither \cmd{?} nor \cmd{=} after it.}
\end{results}
\end{command}

rest of the line is not read.
\begin{results}
\res{\rc{OK}}{Always.}
\end{results}
\end{command}

\section{Dialling, answering and hanging up}\label{sec:dial}

\begin{command}{D}{D\p{string}}{Dial}
This command takes the modem off hook and dials the number in
\p{string}, then calls.  The digits and \cmd{* \# A B C D} are dialed as
DTMF, each \sreg{11} ms long with \sreg{11} ms between them; \cmdl[T]{T} and
\cmdl[P]{P} choose tone or pulse dialing (\sreg{16}) for the rest of the
call.  Pulses are dialed on an analogue line (dmodemd's \texttt{-a} and
\texttt{--usb}), with the country's break, make, gap between digits
and digit pattern (\cmdl[plusGCI]{+GCI}; \cmd{* \# A B C D} skipped); a
line that cannot pulse, such as SIP, dials them in DTMF.  With
\cmdl[X]{X2} or \cmdl[X]{X4} the modem waits for a dial tone first.  In
dmodemd the dial string is the SIP call's number
(\hyperref[sec:sip]{Section~\ref*{sec:sip}}).

\begin{vtable}{2}{|C{0.12\textwidth}|L{0.74\textwidth}|}{Dial Modifiers}
  {\thd{Modifier} & \thd{Function}}
0--9 * \# A--D & Dialed as DTMF, or as pulses for the digits with \cmdl[P]{P}. \\ \hline
L & Dial the last number again (\rc{Dialing \p{number}...} first).
  Valid only as the first character of the string; \rc{ERROR} while a call
  is held after \cmdl[D]{;}. \\ \hline
T & Tone dialing (DTMF) from here on. \\ \hline
P & Pulse dialing from here on. \\ \hline
, & Pause for \sreg{8} seconds. \\ \hline
W & Wait for a dial tone (1 s of it): \rc{NO DIALTONE} if none comes in \sreg{7}
  seconds. \\ \hline
@ & Wait for the remote end's ringing (0.5 s of sound at least), then
  for 5 s of quiet after it: \rc{NO ANSWER} if that does not come in \sreg{7}
  seconds. \\ \hline
; & End the dial string: once the number is dialed the modem is back in
  command state, off hook with the call kept, and says \rc{OK}; the rest of
  the line runs then. \\ \hline
S=\p{n} & A stored number: \rc{ERROR} (there are none). \\ \hline
! \$ & Hook flash and bong tone: skipped (a SIP call has no hook to flash). \\ \hline
\end{vtable}

Other characters are skipped.  After a \cmdl[D]{;} the call waits, silent,
for the DTE: \cmdl[D]{ATD}\p{more} dials more on it (as DTMF; a \cmdl[D]{;}
again holds it again), \cmdl[D]{ATD} alone or \cmdl[O]{ATO} starts the
calling modem's handshake, \cmdl[A]{ATA} the answering modem's, and
\cmdl[H]{ATH} hangs up (\rc{OK}, then \rc{NO CARRIER}).
\begin{results}
\res{\rc{CONNECT}}{When the call is up.}
\res{\rc{OK}}{After a \cmdl[D]{;}.}
\res{\rc{BUSY}, \rc{NO ANSWER}, \rc{NO DIALTONE} or \rc{NO CARRIER}}{When it is not.}
\res{\rc{ERROR}}{For \cmdl[D]{S=}\p{n}, or \cmdl[D]{L} with no number to dial.}
\end{results}
\end{command}

\begin{command}{hashCID}{\#CID, +VCID}{Caller ID}
This command turns caller ID on or off.  With it on, the modem tells
the DTE who is calling: after the first \rc{RING} it sends the date and
time, the caller's number and, when there is one, the caller's name, a
line each.  \cmdl[hashCID]{+VCID} is another name for the same command.

Caller ID works on a SIP call and on a telephone line.  When the caller
has withheld the number or name, the modem sends \rc{P} (private) in its
place; when it is not known, \rc{O} (out of area).  Value 2 sends the
whole caller ID message in hexadecimal instead, for programs that
decode it themselves.

\begin{syntax}
\item AT\#CID=\p{n} (or AT+VCID=\p{n})
\item AT\#CID?
\item AT\#CID=? (gives \rc{0,1,2})
\end{syntax}
\begin{results}
\res{\rc{OK}}{If \p{n} = 0--2.}
\res{\rc{ERROR}}{Otherwise.}
\end{results}
\begin{vtable}{2}{|C{0.16\textwidth}|L{0.7\textwidth}|}{\#CID=\p{n} Values}
  {\thd{Value} & \thd{Function}}
0 & Off (default). \\ \hline
1 & The fields: \rc{DATE = }, \rc{TIME = }, \rc{NMBR = }, \rc{NAME = },

\example{Example output: AT\#CID=1, then a call}
\begin{verbatim}

TIME = 1043
NMBR = +15551234

RING
\end{verbatim}
\par\needspace{4\baselineskip}Technical details:
\begin{itemize}
\item \rc{DATE} is the month and day (MMDD), \rc{TIME} the hour and
  minute (HHMM).
\item In voice mode (\cmdl[plusFCLASS]{+FCLASS=8}) the lines come as \std{V.253}'s
  complex event report, between \cmd{<DLE>X} and \cmd{<DLE>.}; in the
  other modes after an empty line.
\item On a telephone line the modem listens for caller ID after the
  first ring (North America's way, and ETSI's after a short ring).  On a
  line it hears on hook (ALSA, a pipe, the pico-serial Line) it also
  listens before the ring (ETSI's after its alerting tone, the UK's,
  DTMF caller ID).
\item It takes Bell 202 FSK and \std{V.23} FSK (ETSI EN 300 659-1, BT SIN 227)
  alike, and DTMF caller ID: \rc{A} and the number, or \rc{B} (or
  \rc{D}) and a reason it is absent (\cmd{10} private, as \rc{P};
  others \rc{O}), up to \rc{C}, \cmd{\#} or a pause.
\item In voice mode with the line taken it takes Type~II caller ID too,
  a waiting call's caller: after the CPE alerting signal (2130 and
  2750~Hz) it answers with DTMF \rc{A} (GR-30's, a CPE without ADSI)
  and reports the FSK message that follows as a packet, the line's
  audio to the DTE silent meanwhile.
\item A SIP call (dmodemd \cmd{-L}) has its caller ID from the INVITE:
  the caller's P-Asserted-Identity, else its From (the URI's user part
  as \rc{NMBR}, the display name as \rc{NAME}), the date and time the
  host's.  A withheld number or name (the Privacy header's \cmd{id},
  \cmd{user} or \cmd{header}, or an anonymous caller) is \rc{P}; with
  value 2 the modem sends the MDMF message it made of them.
\end{itemize}
\end{command}

\begin{command}{ampL}{\&L\p{n}}{Leased Line}
This command sets whether the line is a dialled one or a leased pair or
circuit, with no exchange: no dial tone, ringing or hook (\sreg{47}).
On a leased line \cmdl[D]{ATD} (any number, or none) calls at once,
listening only for the answer tone; \cmdl[A]{ATA} answers; with
\sreg{0} above 0 the modem answers by itself, with no ring, whenever it
is idle.  A call the line ends (\rc{NO CARRIER}) starts again by itself 2~s
later, dialled or answered as before; \cmdl[H]{ATH}, DTR,
\cmdl[Z]{ATZ} or \cmdl[ampL]{\&L0} stop that until the next command line.

Every voice band modulation of \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS} runs on \cmdl[ampL]{\&L1}.
On \cmdl[ampL]{\&L2} the 4-wire modulations (\cmdl[plusMS]{+MS=V29}, \cmd{V33},
\cmd{V27TC}) run as duplex pumps on a continuous carrier, each end
starting at once with its own synchronizing signal; the others run as
on \cmdl[ampL]{\&L1}.  With \sreg{48} = 1, on a call with no error correction,
the modem does \std{V.13}'s simulated carrier control: between the DTE's
bursts of data the line carries \std{V.13}'s idle pattern, which the far end
takes for carrier detect OFF, and each burst turns it ON again.  A line
at 48000 Hz and more (dmodemd's \cmd{-r 96000}) is always leased: its
mode is \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS=VWB}, and its start-up and retrains take
\sreg{7} seconds at most.

\paragraph{The 4-wire modulations.}  \std{V.29}, \std{V.33} and \std{V.27ter} are the
4-wire leased line modulations (\cmdl[ampL]{\&L2}): each end sends on
its own pair, on a carrier that stays on.  \p{max} sets the rate each
end sends at, \p{min} the lowest it comes down to: a receiver finds the
far end's rate by itself, an end faster than the far end comes down to
its rate, and one that loses its equalization twice in 20~s at its own
rate takes the next rate down.  A receiver that loses its equalization
sends its synchronizing signal again and gets the far end's back (\std{V.29}
10, \std{V.33} 9).

\paragraph{V.90, \std{V.91} and \std{V.92}.}  \std{V.90} and \std{V.92} are there as the
\emph{digital} modem (the server's side, as on a \std{G.711} VoIP line), on
an 8000 Hz \std{G.711} line: analogue \std{V.90} and \std{V.92} modems calling connect at
up to 56000 bit/s downstream.  90 and 92 offer \std{V.91} too, which two
digital modems use instead, and \std{V.34} for the others; on a line at
another sample rate they are \std{V.34}.  With automode \std{V.8} and the \std{V.34}
modem hand over to \std{V.32bis} (and its \std{V.22bis} and \std{V.22}) for a modem
without \std{V.34}, and the answering modem tries \std{V.23} and Bell 103 for one
without \std{V.8}.

\paragraph{On an analogue line.}\label{sec:analogue}  On an analogue
telephone line (dmodemd's \texttt{-a} and \texttt{--usb}) the modem is
the call's analogue end instead: \std{V.90} and \std{V.92} run as the analogue
modem against a \std{V.90} or \std{V.92} server (at 9600~Hz; see
\texttt{docs/hardware.md}), and \std{V.91} and \cmd{CLEAR}, which need a
digital line, run as \std{V.34}.

\paragraph{V.cvm.}  \cmd{VCVM} is the proposed Recommendation of
\cmd{docs/v\_cvm.pdf}, for calls whose speech path is a mobile codec's
(AMR, AMR-WB), which carry neither \std{V.34} nor \std{V.32}: \std{V.8} with \std{V.22} and \std{V.21}
as the fallback, then a probing of the codec and a mode loaded for it.
The start-up takes 15 to 21~s; \sreg{7} should allow for it.  The
connection runs LAPM.  \cmd{V\_CVM}, its old name, is still taken.

\paragraph{V.wb.}  \cmd{VWB} is D-Modem's own mode for a bare copper
pair between two of its modems (pico-serial's WB stick, a codec at 96
kHz): discrete multitone, the caller on the lower tones and the
answerer on the upper, each tone loaded from its measured SNR; see
\cmd{docs/dsp/vwb.md}.  It runs at 48000 Hz and more only, where it is
the only mode and the default (\cmdl[Z]{ATZ} sets it again there), as a
leased line (\cmdl[ampL]{\&L1}); the rates of \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS} do not limit it.

\paragraph{Clear channel.}  \cmd{CLEAR} is a clear 64 kbit/s digital
channel, such as an ISDN B-channel's data call (a SIP call in
CLEARMODE, RFC 4040, or a PBX's call with an unrestricted digital
bearer): no modulation, each of the channel's octets 8 bits of the
link's, \rc{CONNECT 64000} both ways.  It starts at once on \cmdl[D]{ATD}
and \cmdl[A]{ATA}, with no call progress and no answer tone.  Each octet's
first bit is its most significant (\std{I.460}, RFC 4040).  A line that
says its call is clear (dmodemd's SIP for a CLEARMODE call,
\cmd{app\_dmodem} for an ISDN data call) runs it whatever \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS}
says.  What runs on the channel is \cmdl[plusB2]{+B2}'s, \std{X.75} by
default, or \std{V.110}'s rate adaption with \cmdl[plusCBST]{+CBST}.  See
\cmd{docs/isdn.md}.
\end{command}

\subsection{V.80 synchronous access}\label{sec:v80}

With \cmdl[plusES]{+ES=6} (calling) or \cmdl[plusES]{+ES=,,8} (answering) the
call has no error correction or compression: after \rc{CONNECT} the modem
moves the DTE's octets as a synchronous bit stream, with \std{V.80}'s in-band
commands after EM (19h) both ways (Table~9/V.80).

It starts in the \emph{transparent} sub-mode: the DTE's octets go out as
they are, least significant bit first, and the line's bits come back as
octets; when the DTE has nothing the modem sends \p{syn1} and tells it
\cmd{<EM><under>}.  With \cmdl[plusESA]{+ESA}'s \p{trans\_idle} 1 or 2
the receiver first hunts for the \std{SYN}, and gives the DTE nothing before
it.  \cmd{<EM><flag>} from the DTE starts the \emph{framed} sub-mode:
HDLC frames, each ended by the DTE's \cmd{<EM><flag>}, the modem adding
the zero insertion, the flags and the FCS.  It gives back each received
frame's octets (without the FCS it checked) and \cmd{<EM><flag>} for a
good one or \cmd{<EM><err>} for a bad one, an \cmd{<EM><err>} when its
hunt finds a flag, and \cmd{<EM><mark>} for an abort or marks.
\cmd{<EM><mark>} from the DTE goes back to transparent.  EM and 99h in

\subsection{S-register definitions}

The range given is what the register means; \cmdl[S]{ATS} takes any value
from 0 to 255 for any register but S44 and S45, which are read only.
\cmdl[Z]{ATZ} sets the registers to their defaults, but for S18, S28, S47,
S48 and S161, which keep their values.

\sregdef{0}{Auto-Answer Ring Number}
The number of rings the modem counts before it answers a call by
itself.  0 disables auto-answer: the modem then answers only with
\cmdl[A]{ATA}.  On a leased line (\cmdl[ampL]{\&L}) a value above 0
makes the modem answer, with no ring, whenever it is idle.
\facts{0--255}{0}{rings}

\sregdef{1}{Ring Counter}
The rings counted so far: \sreg{0} compares it with its own value.
\facts{0--255}{0}{rings}

\sregdef{2}{Escape Character}
The ASCII value of the character of the escape sequence, \cmdr{+++}
(\hyperref[sec:online]{Section~\ref*{sec:online}}).
\facts{0--255}{43 (\cmd{+})}{ASCII}

\sregdef{3}{Command Line Termination Character}
The character that ends a command line and the result codes.
\facts{0--255}{13 (carriage return)}{ASCII}

\sregdef{4}{Response Formatting Character}
The line feed after the result codes; a command line ends at it as well.
\facts{0--255}{10 (line feed)}{ASCII}

\sregdef{5}{Command Line Editing Character}
The backspace: it takes back the last character typed.  With echo on,
the modem echoes it, a space and the backspace again.
\facts{0--255}{8 (backspace)}{ASCII}

\sregdef{6}{Wait Time for Dial Tone}
With \cmdl[X]{X2} or \cmdl[X]{X4}, how long the modem waits for a dial
tone before \rc{NO DIALTONE}: 2 s at least whatever the value, and 2 s more
while a dial tone in a cadence is heard.
\facts{0--255}{2}{seconds}

\sregdef{7}{Connection Completion Time-Out}
How long the modem waits for the call to come up after dialing or
answering: the answer and the training.  The dial string's \cmdl[D]{W} and
\cmdl[D]{@} wait this long too, and voice mode's \cmdl[plusVRN]{+VRN} no longer.
\std{V.cvm}'s start-up takes 15 to 21~s, so S7 should allow for it.
\facts{0--255}{60}{seconds}

\sregdef{8}{Pause Time for Comma Dial Modifier}
The pause of a \cmd{,} in the dial string.  The country (\cmdl[plusGCI]{+GCI})
sets it.
\facts{0--255}{2 (the country's)}{seconds}

\sregdef{9}{Carrier Detect Response Time}
Kept: the modulations detect the carrier by themselves.
\facts{0--255}{6}{0.1 s}

\sregdef{10}{Automatic Disconnect Delay}
How long the carrier may be gone before the modem hangs up with \rc{NO CARRIER}.
\facts{0--255}{7}{0.1 s}

\sregdef{11}{DTMF Dialing Speed}
The length of each DTMF digit dialed, and of the gap after it.  A value
below 40 dials at 95.
\facts{0--255}{100}{milliseconds}

\sregdef{12}{Escape Guard Time}
Kept: the escape's guard time is always 0.5 s.
\facts{0--255}{50}{0.02 s}

\sregdef{13}{Echo}
\cmdl[E]{E}'s setting.
\facts{0, 1}{1}{none}

\sregdef{14}{Quiet}
\cmdl[Q]{Q}'s setting.
\facts{0, 1}{0}{none}

\sregdef{15}{Verbose}
\cmdl[V]{V}'s setting.
\facts{0, 1}{1}{none}

\sregdef{16}{Tone or Pulse Dialing}
1 tone (\cmdl[T]{T}), 0 pulse (\cmdl[P]{P}).
\facts{0, 1}{1}{none}

\sregdef{18}{Loop Test Timer}
A loop test (\cmdl[ampT]{\&T}) ends after this many seconds; 0, never.
\cmdl[Z]{ATZ} keeps it.
\facts{0--255}{0}{seconds}

\sregdef{24}{Hook Flash Time}
The length of a hook flash.  The country sets it.
\facts{0--255}{20 (the country's)}{0.01 s}

\sregdef{28}{Pulse Dial Ratio}
\cmdl[ampP]{\&P}'s setting, kept: the country's table sets the pulse
times.  \cmdl[Z]{ATZ} keeps it.
\facts{0--3}{0}{none}

\sregdef{29}{Speaker Control}
\cmdl[M]{M}'s setting.
\facts{0--3}{1}{none}

\cmdl[L]{L}'s setting.

\section{Fax commands}\label{sec:fax}

With \cmdl[plusFCLASS]{AT+FCLASS=1}, the modem takes \std{T.31}'s \std{Class 1}
commands, the fax machine's side being the DTE's (the PBX modules and
libdmodem have a \std{T.30} engine of their own as well).  As the caller, the
modem sends \std{CNG} after dialing until the answerer's flags come in its
first \cmdl[plusFTH]{+FRH=3} (\std{T.30} has it until the \std{DIS}).

\begin{command}{plusFCLASS}{+FCLASS}{Service Class}
This command sets whether the modem is a data modem, a \std{Class 1}, \std{Class 2} or
2.0 fax modem or a voice modem.  \cmdl[Z]{ATZ} sets 0.
\begin{syntax}
\item +FCLASS=\p{n}
\item +FCLASS? (gives the class)
\item +FCLASS=? (gives \rc{0,1,2,2.0,8})
\end{syntax}
\begin{results}
\res{\rc{OK}}{If \p{n} = 0, 1, 2, 2.0 or 8.}
\res{\rc{ERROR}}{Otherwise.}
\end{results}
\begin{vtable}{2}{|C{0.08\textwidth}|L{0.78\textwidth}|}{+FCLASS Values}
  {\thd{Value} & \thd{Class}}
0 & Data (default). \\ \hline
1 & Fax \std{Class 1} (\std{T.31}). \\ \hline
2 & Fax \std{Class 2} (\hyperref[sec:class2]{Section~\ref*{sec:class2}}). \\ \hline
2.0 & Fax \std{Class 2.0} (\std{T.32}, \hyperref[sec:class2]{Section~\ref*{sec:class2}}). \\ \hline
8 & Voice (\std{V.253}, \hyperref[sec:voice]{Section~\ref*{sec:voice}}). \\ \hline
\end{vtable}
\end{command}

\begin{command}{plusFAA}{+FAA}{Adaptive Answer}
With \cmdl[plusFAA]{+FAA=1}, a call the modem answers (\cmdl[A]{ATA}, \sreg{0}) in
data or fax mode is answered as a data call (its answer tone, \std{V.8}'s
ANSam, serves a fax machine as its \std{CED}), and found out to be a fax
machine's or a data modem's:
\begin{itemize}
\item a fax call by the caller's \std{CNG} (1100~Hz, 0.5~s of every 3.5~s;
  or a burst cut short as the answer tone starts, a fax machine that
  stops its \std{CNG} on hearing it), the \std{V.8} \std{CM} of a fax call (a call
  function of facsimile, as \std{V.34} fax machines and some \std{Class 1} modems
  send it on ANSam), the far end going over to \std{T.38} (dmodemd's SIP
  calls), or a caller silent through every answer signal of the
  automode chain (ANSam, then \std{V.32}'s, \std{V.22}'s, \std{V.21}'s, \std{V.23}'s and Bell
  103's, some 20~s, within \std{T.30}'s T1 of 35~s): \rc{FAX},
  \cmdl[plusFCLASS]{+FCLASS} becomes 1, and the modem goes on as a
  \std{Class 1} answerer: what is left of \std{CED}'s 3~s, then \rc{CONNECT}, as after
  \cmdl[plusFTH]{AT+FTH=3}, for the DTE's first frames;
\item a data call by its modem's handshake: \rc{DATA} when it comes up, then
  its \rc{CONNECT}; \cmdl[plusFCLASS]{+FCLASS} becomes 0.
\end{itemize}
A call that ends with neither leaves \cmdl[plusFCLASS]{+FCLASS} as it was.  A \std{V.22},
\std{V.21} or Bell 103 caller, silent until it hears its own answer signal,
answers its stage of the chain: \rc{DATA}.  In voice mode see
\hyperref[sec:voice]{Section~\ref*{sec:voice}}: a calling tone on the call switches it to fax
or data.  Rockwell's modems give \rc{FAX} and \rc{DATA} too, as 33 and 35 with
\cmdl[V]{V0}: here those are rates' CONNECTs, so 88 and 89.
\begin{syntax}
\item +FAA=\p{n}
\item +FAA? (gives \rc{+FAA: 0})
\item +FAA=? (gives \rc{+FAA: (0,1)})
\end{syntax}
\begin{results}
\res{\rc{OK}}{If \p{n} = 0 or 1.}
\res{\rc{ERROR}}{Otherwise.}
\end{results}

  {\thd{Value} & \thd{Meaning}}
0 & Answer as \cmdl[plusFCLASS]{+FCLASS} says (default; \cmdl[Z]{Z} sets it). \\ \hline
1 & Adaptive answer: fax or data. \\ \hline
\end{vtable}
\end{command}

\subsection{Class 2 and 2.0}\label{sec:class2}

With \cmdl[plusFCLASS]{AT+FCLASS=2.0} (\std{T.32}) or \cmdl[plusFCLASS]{AT+FCLASS=2} (the
older \std{Class 2} that fax programs know), the modem runs \std{T.30} itself: the
DTE gives and takes the pages in \std{T.4} MH with \std{EOL}s, \cmd{<DLE>} doubled,
ended by \cmd{<DLE><ETX>}, and steers the call with the commands below.
\std{Class 2} has the older names: \cmdr{+FLID}, \cmdr{+FDCC}, \cmdr{+FDIS},
\cmdr{+FDCS}, \cmdr{+FBOR}, \cmdr{+FK}, \cmdr{+FPTS} and \cmdr{+FHNG} (either
name works in either class), and its reports say \rc{+FCON},
\rc{+FCSI}, \rc{+FTSI}, \rc{+FDIS}, \rc{+FDCS}, \rc{+FPTS} and
\rc{+FHNG}.  The rates are \std{V.17}, \std{V.29} and \std{V.27ter}'s; error correction
mode, when \cmdl[plusFCC]{+FIS} has it, is the modem's own (the DTE's data is the
same).  The pages are MH whatever the remote uses (MR or ECM: the modem
codes them again), A4 width.  A call goes over to \std{T.38} as a \std{Class 1}
call does (the same commands and results).  \cmdl[plusFAA]{+FAA}
(adaptive answer) is \std{Class 1}'s, and \std{Class 1}'s \cmdl[plusFTH]{+FTH}, \cmdl[plusFTH]{+FRH},
\cmdl[plusFTM]{+FTM}, \cmdl[plusFTM]{+FRM}, \cmdl[plusFTS]{+FTS} and \cmdl[plusFTS]{+FRS} are \rc{ERROR} in \std{Class 2}
mode.

\example{Example session: sending two pages, then receiving them}
\begin{verbatim}
ATD<number>       +FCO           the DIS came
                  +FCI:"<id>"
                  +FIS:1,5,0,2,0,0,0,0
                  OK
AT+FDT            +FCS:1,5,0,2,0,0,0,0
                  CONNECT        DCS, training, CFR
<page><DLE><ETX>  OK
AT+FET=0          +FPS:1,0,0,0,0
                  OK             MPS, MCF
AT+FDT            CONNECT
<page><DLE><ETX>  OK
AT+FET=2          +FPS:1,0,0,0,0
                  +FHS:00
                  OK             EOP, MCF, DCN

ATA               +FCO           the CFR went out
                  +FTI:"<id>"
                  +FCS:1,5,0,2,0,0,0,0
                  OK
AT+FDR            CONNECT
<DC2>             <page><DLE><ETX>
                  +FPS:1,<lines>,0,0,0
                  +FET:0
                  OK
AT+FDR            ... +FET:2, OK
AT+FDR            +FHS:00
                  OK
\end{verbatim}

In \std{Class 2} an XON follows the sending \rc{CONNECT}.  \cmdl[plusFDT]{+FDR}'s page waits
for the DTE's \cmd{<DC2>} (a second at most); the pages are taken and
confirmed as they come, and wait for \cmdl[plusFDT]{+FDR}.  When the call ends,
the modem reports the hangup status and the result of the command then
under way (\rc{NO CARRIER} for \cmdl[D]{ATD} and \cmdl[A]{ATA}), and goes on hook.

\begin{vtable}{2}{|C{0.08\textwidth}|L{0.78\textwidth}|}{+FHS Hangup Status}
  {\thd{Value} & \thd{Meaning}}
00 & The call was done. \\ \hline
02 & \cmdl[plusFDT]{+FKS} ended it. \\ \hline
11 & No fax machine answered. \\ \hline
2x & Sending, phase B (the \std{DIS}, the training). \\ \hline
4x, 5x & Sending a page, or the post-page exchange. \\ \hline
7x, 9x & Receiving. \\ \hline
\end{vtable}
\std{Class 2.0} gives the status in hexadecimal digits (\std{T.32} Table 23),
\std{Class 2} in decimal.

\begin{command}{plusFLI}{+FLI, +FPI}{Local ID, Polling ID}
Our identity in \std{TSI} and \std{CSI} (\cmdl[plusFLI]{+FLI}, \std{Class 2}'s \cmdr{+FLID}) and in
\std{CIG} when we poll (\cmdl[plusFLI]{+FPI}, \std{Class 2}'s \rc{+FCIG}; empty: \cmdl[plusFLI]{+FLI}'s):

\section{Text telephone commands}\label{sec:text}

The modem is a text telephone in \std{V.18}'s modes (\cmdl[plusMS]{+MS} or
\cmdl[plusMV18S]{+MV18S} chooses one): the 5-bit (Baudot) mode of the
TTYs, at 45.45~bit/s on 1400 and 1800~Hz (TIA-825-A), and with
\cmdl[plusMBAUDOT]{+MBAUDOT} the telex and RTTY variants (50~bit/s,
ITA2's figures, other tones); EDT (\std{V.21}'s channel~1 at 110~bit/s, 7
bits, even parity, 2 stop bits); and \std{V.21}, \std{V.23} and Bell 103 as their
data modes.  The 5-bit mode and EDT are half duplex: the modem sends
150~ms of mark, the characters, and 300~ms of mark after the last, and
does not hear the line meanwhile; it starts no characters while the
remote's carrier is on.  Neither hangs up for want of carrier.  The DTE
sends and receives ASCII: in the 5-bit mode lower case goes as capitals,
the shift codes are the modem's, and characters the code has not are
dropped.  A text telephone call has no answer tone: dialling, the modem
goes into its mode as the dialling ends; answering, at once.

\cmdl[plusMS]{AT+MS=V18} (or \cmdl[plusMV18S]{AT+MV18S=1}) finds the remote's mode.  It
listens for 3~s, for a 5-bit character, an EDT character, \std{V.21}'s
channel~1 at 300~bit/s (or a steady mark), a Bell 103 or \std{V.23} caller's
mark (answering), or \std{V.21}'s channel~2, Bell 103's 2225~Hz or \std{V.23}'s
1300~Hz (calling).  Hearing nothing it probes, in turn: the answer
message (\cmdl[plusMV18AM]{+MV18AM}) and a new line in the 5-bit mode,
then in EDT, each followed by 2~s of listening, then 3~s each of its own
\std{V.21}, Bell 103 and \std{V.23} mark, listening for the answer to it; and over
again until \sreg{7} runs out.  Found, a 5-bit or EDT remote is answered
in its mode (once it is quiet, so that another automoding modem, whose
probe this may have been, finds it too); \std{V.21}, \std{V.23} and Bell 103 go on
as those modems do.  DTMF text telephones (\std{V.18}'s DTMF mode) are not
supported.

\subsection{Voice events}

While the line is taken (\cmdl[plusVLS]{+VLS=1}) the modem reports what it hears,
as \std{V.253} has it, as \cmd{<DLE>} and a character.  DTMF digits from the
far end are reported 51~ms into them and again every 70~ms while they
last, in the voice command state as well as among \cmdl[plusVTX]{+VRX}'s samples,
whose audio is silence from a digit's report until it ends; the modem's
own \cmdl[plusVTS]{+VTS} tones are not reported.  The calling tones are reported
as a burst ends and 4~s apart at least.

\begin{vtable}{2}{|T{0.2\textwidth}|L{0.66\textwidth}|}{Voice Events}
  {\thd{Report} & \thd{Event}}
<DLE>0--9 * \# A--D & A DTMF digit from the far end. \\ \hline
<DLE>\textasciitilde & The DTMF digit ended. \\ \hline
<DLE>r & A ring of a ringing tone (400 to 480~Hz, or 440 and 480~Hz, or
  400 and 450~Hz, on for 0.6~s at least; the UK's two bursts are one
  ring; it ends after 0.3~s without the tone). \\ \hline
<DLE>b & A busy or congestion tone (four rounds of 0.2 to 0.8~s on and
  off). \\ \hline
<DLE>a & An answer tone of 2100~Hz (a fax or data answer), 0.3~s into
  it. \\ \hline
<DLE>f & An answer tone of 2225~Hz (a data answer), 0.3~s into it. \\ \hline
<DLE>c & A calling fax machine's \std{CNG} (1100~Hz, bursts of 0.36 to
  0.8~s). \\ \hline
<DLE>e & A calling data modem's \std{V.25} tone (1300~Hz). \\ \hline
<DLE>l & Loop current interruption: the far end ended the call. \\ \hline
<DLE>H, <DLE>h & The phone on the modem's own jack off hook, on hook
  again. \\ \hline
<DLE>X \ldots <DLE>. & Caller ID (\cmdl[hashCID]{+VCID}). \\ \hline
\end{vtable}

When the far end ends a voice call, the modem reports \cmd{<DLE>l}
(\std{V.253}'s loop current interruption, ``usually a remote hangup'') and
goes on hook (\cmdl[plusVLS]{+VLS=0}); \std{V.253} has no \rc{NO CARRIER} in voice mode.
What was under way ends: \cmdl[plusVTX]{+VRX}'s samples with \cmd{<DLE><ETX>},
then \rc{OK}; \cmdl[plusVTX]{+VTX} and \cmdl[plusVTX]{+VTR} when the DTE ends them (its samples
until then are discarded), then \rc{OK}, \cmdl[plusVTX]{+VTR}'s after
\cmd{<DLE><ETX>}; \cmdl[plusVTS]{+VTS}'s tones with \rc{ERROR}.  In the command state
\cmd{<DLE>l} comes alone.

\subsection{From voice to fax or data}

A voice call can become a fax or data call (\std{V.253} 5.3.2 and its
example \std{I.6}).  \cmdl[plusFCLASS]{+FCLASS=1} (or \cmd{0}) in the voice
command state, off hook, keeps the call: the voice engine stops, the
line stays off hook and silent, and \cmdl[A]{A} answers on it as a fax
(\std{Class 1}: \std{CED}, then \rc{CONNECT} for the \std{DIS}) or data modem, or
\cmdl[D]{D} calls on it (call progress waits for the far end's answer
tone).  \cmdl[H]{H}, \cmdl[Z]{Z} or the far end hanging up ends the held
call.  For example, a fax machine calls while the greeting plays:
\example{Example session: from voice to fax}
\begin{verbatim}
AT+VTX                 CONNECT      the greeting's samples follow
                       <DLE>c       the caller's CNG
<DLE><ETX>             OK
AT+FCLASS=1            OK
ATA                    CONNECT      class 1, as after AT+FTH=3
\end{verbatim}
With \cmdl[plusFAA]{+FAA=1} the modem does it itself, in the voice
command state (a \cmdl[plusVTX]{+VTX}, \cmdl[plusVTX]{+VRX}, \cmdl[plusVTX]{+VTR} or \cmdl[plusVTS]{+VTS} under
way ends first): after \cmd{<DLE>c}, \rc{FAX}, \cmdl[plusFCLASS]{+FCLASS=1} and the fax
answer's \rc{CONNECT}; after \cmd{<DLE>e}, \cmdl[plusFCLASS]{+FCLASS=0} and the adaptive
data answer (\rc{DATA}, then its \rc{CONNECT}).

\section{Online, the escape and hanging up}\label{sec:online}

Online, what the DTE sends goes to the other modem, except the escape
sequence: three \sreg{2} characters (\cmdr{+++}), each within 0.5 s of
the one before, with 0.5 s of nothing before them and after them.  The
modem then says \rc{OK} and is in online command mode, the call still up:
\cmdl[O]{ATO} goes back online, \cmdl[H]{ATH} hangs up, and on a \std{V.92}
connection \cmdl[plusPMHR]{AT+PMHR} asks for modem-on-hold.  The
\cmdr{+++} itself is data (the modem cannot know it is the escape until
the guard time after it), and what the DTE sent before the escape and
the line had not taken yet is kept, not dropped: it goes to the other
modem, \cmdr{+++} last, once \cmdl[O]{ATO} is back online.

The call ends with \rc{NO CARRIER} when the other side hangs up or its
carrier is gone for \sreg{10} tenths of a second.  dmodemd hangs up as
well when the DTE drops DTR (it sets the pty's speed to 0) or, with
HUPCL set on the pty, closes it; it then makes a new pty
(\cmd{/dev/ttyDM0} follows it).

\section{dmodemd on a SIP line}\label{sec:sip}

\begin{itemize}
\item \cmdl[D]{ATD}\p{number} calls \cmd{sip:}\p{number}\cmd{@}\p{domain}
  of \cmd{SIP\_LOGIN}.  A telephone number (only \std{V.250}'s dial
  characters: digits, \cmd{* \# + A B C D}, the modifiers \cmd{T P W , @
  ! ;} and \cmd{- ( ) .} and blanks) goes as its digits and DTMF
  characters: \cmdl[D]{ATDT555-1000} calls \cmd{sip:5551000@}\p{domain}.
  Anything else is a SIP user's name, as it is: \cmdl[D]{ATDalice}.
\item The line has no dial tone: \cmdl[X]{ATX3} (or \cmdl[X]{X1}), else \rc{NO DIALTONE}.
\item A dial string with a \cmdl[D]{;} (\cmdl[D]{ATDT5551000;}) makes the SIP
  call and says \rc{OK} at once, the modem in command state: what \cmdl[D]{ATD}
  adds then goes as DTMF on the call (a menu's choices, an extension),
  and \cmdl[D]{ATD} alone or \cmdl[A]{ATA} starts the modem's handshake.
\item With \cmd{-L} dmodemd registers and takes calls: \rc{RING} every 3
  seconds while one waits, \cmdl[A]{ATA} or \sreg{0} rings
  (\cmdl[S]{ATS0=1}) answer it, and the rings stop if the caller gives up.
  A call that comes while the line is in use is refused (486, busy);
  without \cmd{-L} every call is (480).
\item The call is \std{G.711} at 8000 Hz, which is what \std{V.90} and \std{V.92} need
  (\cmdl[plusMS]{AT+MS=92}, the default).  Over a real PSTN gateway a
  lower modulation (\cmdl[plusMS]{AT+MS=132,0,4800,9600}) may do better.
\end{itemize}

\section{Compatibility}\label{sec:compat}

\subsection{Where D-Modem differs}

\begin{itemize}
\item \cmdl[ampK]{\&K} is the compression, as on USRobotics' modems;
  on Rockwell's and Conexant's it is flow control.  \cmdl[ampK]{\&K3} leaves
  \std{V.42}'s compression on (\std{MNP~5} off), \cmdl[ampK]{\&K0} turns it off.
\item The S-registers past \sreg{16} are D-Modem's own: \sreg{29} and
  \sreg{30} are the speaker's (elsewhere the hook flash time and the
  inactivity timer), \sreg{92} the answer's delay (elsewhere a fax
  transmit level).  The others (S36, S37, S91 \ldots) are free: kept
  as written.  \sreg{7} and \sreg{11} start at 60 and 100.
\item There are no stored numbers or profiles: \cmdr{\&Z} and
  \cmdl[D]{ATDS=}\p{n} are \rc{ERROR}, \cmdl[ampW]{\&W} stores nothing.
\item \cmdl[I]{ATI}'s numbers are the modem's own (\cmdl[I]{I0} to
  \cmdl[I]{I7}), but for the call statistics: \cmdl[I]{I6} as USRobotics',
  \cmdl[I]{I11} as Conexant's, \cmdl[ampV]{\&V1} as Rockwell's.
\item The numeric results (\cmdl[V]{V0}) and \rc{CONNECT}'s number for
  each rate are D-Modem's own; those of USRobotics' and Conexant's
  modems differ.
\item \std{MNP} is classes 2 to 5 (\cmdl[bsN]{\bs N}, \cmdl[pctC]{\%C}): there
  is no \std{MNP~10} or 10EC.  A modem with \std{MNP~10EC} connects with \std{V.42} (its
  ``EM'' detection patterns are passed over).  See
  \texttt{docs/link.md}, \std{MNP}.
\item K56flex is not there: \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS=56} runs \std{V.90}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Not supported}

These are \rc{ERROR}: \cmdl[ampV]{\&V2} and up, \cmdr{\&Z} (no stored numbers),
\cmdl[ampT]{\&T2} and \cmdl[ampT]{\&T9} and up, \cmdr{\bs B} (a break), \cmdl[X]{X5} and
up, \cmdl[I]{ATI8} to \cmdl[I]{ATI10} and \cmdl[I]{ATI12} and up, \cmd{-V90}
(\cmdl[plusMS]{+MS} chooses), \cmdr{+MA}, \cmdr{+MSC}, \cmdr{+EB},
\cmdr{+ESR}, \cmdr{+ETBM}, \cmdr{+EWIND}, \cmdr{+EFRAM}, \cmdr{+A8E},
\cmdr{+A8T}, \cmdr{+PMHF}, \cmdr{+GSN} and \cmdr{+GOI}; \cmdl[plusMS]{+MS}'s names of
modulations the modem has not got (\cmd{V23S}, \cmd{V26B}, \cmd{V26T},
\cmd{V27} \ldots).
