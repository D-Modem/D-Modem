\subsection{Signal set}\label{sec:modes}
The signal set has four modes, Table~\ref{tab:modes}.  A DCE shall
support the robust mode and the narrowband mode; it should support the
parametric mode, which alone carries data through the lower codec modes
and carries the messages of clause~\ref{sec:infomod} between DCEs that
have it; it may support the wideband mode (which needs the signal
interface at 16000~Hz).

\itunote{The modes were chosen from measurements (Appendix~\ref{app:measurements}).
Through AMR at 7.4~\kbs{} and below, no differential phase modulation
measured kept a symbol error ratio below $10^{-1}$, on one carrier or many;
only 300~bit/s FSK did.  Through AMR at 12.2 and AMR-WB at 23.85~\kbs{},
a comb of 16 tones at 200~Hz spacing with DQPSK and a symbol of 20 to
22.5~ms kept its tone symbol error ratio at or below $10^{-2}$; combs of
32 tones at 100~Hz spacing did not (the codec's noise against each tone
grows faster than the number of tones), and 8-DPSK held only on the
better tones.  Fewer tones, more widely spaced, keep working where more
do not: through AMR at 10.2~\kbs{} and through two AMR codecs in tandem
the 11-tone comb at 300~Hz spacing kept $3 \cdot 10^{-3}$ while the
16-tone comb went to $3 \cdot 10^{-2}$, and through AMR-WB at 15.85 the
300~Hz comb loads 16 tones where the 200~Hz comb loads 10; so the signal
set has three combs for each band and the probing chooses.  A single
carrier at 600~baud (V.22's) passes AMR~12.2 as
well, at 1200~bit/s; the comb carries a third more at the same symbol
error ratio and loads itself to the codec.  The parametric mode came
from the same measurements, which show that the one parameter every
codec mode but AMR 4.75 transmits to the sample is the long-term
predictor's lag (clause~\ref{sec:parametric}, Table~\ref{tab:pitch}).}

\[ f_k = k \cdot \Delta f, \qquad k = k_{\min}, \ldots, k_{\max}, \]
as Table~\ref{tab:combs} gives them.

The narrowband combs N200, N300 and N400 are for the signal interface
at 8000~Hz, the wideband combs W200, W300 and W400 for 16000~Hz, and the
super wideband combs S200, S300 and S400 for 32000~Hz (for the EVS
codec's super wideband modes, Appendix~\ref{app:evs}).  The probing (clause~\ref{sec:probing}) decides which
tones are loaded; the transmitter sends nothing on a tone that is not.
The transmitted signal in the useful part of symbol $n$ is
\[ s_n(t) = A \sum_{k \in \mathcal{L}} \cos\bigl(2\pi f_k t + \varphi_{n,k}\bigr), \qquad 0 \le t < T_u, \]
where $\mathcal{L}$ is the set of loaded tones, $\varphi_{n,k}$ the tone's
phase in this symbol and $A$ the amplitude that gives the level of
clause~\ref{sec:pointp}~b) over the loaded tones (with the amplitude
rings of clause~\ref{sec:qam} the tone's amplitude is $A a_{n,k}$, of the
same mean power).  The useful period $T_u$
is a whole number of cycles of $\Delta f$, so that the tones are
orthogonal over it.

\subsection{Symbol timing and the cyclic prefix}\label{sec:timing}
A symbol consists of a cyclic prefix of $T_g$ followed by the useful part
of $T_u$: the prefix is the last $T_g$ of the useful part, transmitted
first, so that the signal is continuous across each symbol except for the
phase steps at the symbol's start.  The symbol periods are
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{20~ms}, the period of a codec frame, for every comb: $T_u$
  and $T_g$ as Table~\ref{tab:combs} gives them (three cycles of the
  spacing for the 200 and 300~Hz combs, six for the 400~Hz combs), so
  that one symbol's phase steps fall in each frame and the prefix spans
  the codec's transient after them (a subframe of 5~ms, or two for the
  300~Hz combs);
\item \textbf{22.5~ms}, for the N200 comb only: $T_u = 20$~ms (four
  cycles), $T_g = 2.5$~ms, for codecs that gain from the longer integration
  (the probing compares the two).
\end{itemize}
The receiver shall integrate each tone over the useful part only.  A
receiver shall acquire the symbol timing from the cyclic prefix (the
correlation of the signal with itself $T_u$ earlier peaks over the prefix)
and shall re-acquire it within two symbols after a step of the received
timing of up to $\pm 20$~ms, which a jitter buffer's adaptation causes
(Appendix~\ref{app:terminal}).  Differential modulation makes the symbol
after such a step the only one lost.

\subsection{The codec frame and the symbol}\label{sec:align}
The symbols are not aligned to the codec's frames, and no alignment is
signalled: the position of the symbols' phase steps within the frame was
found to make no difference to the tone symbol error ratio
(Appendix~\ref{app:measurements}, Table~\ref{tab:combnb}: the 20~ms
symbol at a frame boundary and 10~ms off it), which the codec's 5~ms
subframes and its long-term predictor explain: the step is coded in the
subframe it falls in, wherever that is.  The 20~ms symbol is chosen for
its rate (12 per cent more than the 22.5~ms symbol) and for its match
with the frame erasures: one erased frame is one symbol.

\subsection{Differential phase modulation}
Each loaded tone carries, per symbol, the phase difference
$\varphi_{n,k} - \varphi_{n-1,k}$, one of four values for DQPSK or eight for
8-DPSK, mapped from the bits as Table~\ref{tab:gray} shows.  The first
symbol of phase~4, and the first after a renegotiation, carries no data:
it is the phase reference.  The receiver decides each tone symbol from the
difference of the tone's phases in two consecutive symbols; no carrier
recovery and no equalization are required, and a frequency offset between
the DCEs' clocks of $\Delta f_c$ appears as a constant phase rotation of
$2\pi \Delta f_c T$ per symbol, which the receiver should estimate from
the decisions and remove.

\subsection{Loading and raw rate}
The raw rate is
\[ R = \frac{1}{T_u + T_g} \sum_{k \in \mathcal{L}} b_k, \]
with $b_k = 2$ for a DQPSK tone and 3 for an 8-DPSK tone, and one or two
more with the amplitude rings of clause~\ref{sec:qam}: 1600~bit/s for the
16 DQPSK tones of N200 at the 20~ms symbol, 1100 for N300, 800 for N400,
3300 for W200, and half as much again with 8-DPSK on all tones.  The loading of clause~\ref{sec:loading} chooses $\mathcal{L}$
and the $b_k$; a loading with fewer than 4 tones shall not be used (the
DCE takes the parametric mode, clause~\ref{sec:loading}~e)).

\subsection{The parametric mode}\label{sec:parametric}
\subsubsection{Principle}
The parametric mode carries the data not in a waveform but in a
parameter the codec must transmit to describe the signal at all: the
period of a periodic signal, which an ACELP codec codes as the lag of
its long-term predictor in every subframe, at its lowest modes as at its
highest (clause~\ref{sec:general}~h)).  The transmitter sends a pulse
train whose period is the symbol; the decoder reproduces a signal with
that period; the receiver measures it.  Neither DCE touches the codec or
its bitstream, and the mode is a signal at the interface of
clause~\ref{sec:pointp} like the others.

\subsubsection{Signal}
In symbol $n$, of $T$ samples, the transmitter sends pulses $L_n$ samples
apart, the first at the symbol's start:
\[ e(m) = \sum_{j \ge 0,\; jL_n < T} \delta(m - nT - jL_n), \qquad
   x(m) = e(m) + 0.7\,x(m-1), \qquad s = A\,(x * h), \]
where $L_n$ is the symbol's lag from the alphabet of
Table~\ref{tab:lags}; the one-pole filter gives the pulses the spectral
tilt of a glottal pulse (about $-6$~dB per octave above 400~Hz, so that
the codec's classifier takes the signal for voiced speech); and $h$ is a
band-pass pulse of 33 taps at 8000~Hz, the impulse response of an ideal
band-pass from 300~Hz to the top of the codec's band (3400~Hz at the
8000~Hz interface) under a Hamming window.  $A$ sets the level:
$-15 \pm 2$~dBFS rms over a symbol; the signal's peak factor is 13~dB,
and its peaks shall stay below $-1$~dBFS (clause~\ref{sec:pointp}~b)).
At the 16000~Hz interface the lags and the pulse have twice the samples,
so that the periods in time are the same.

Each alphabet has 16 lags: four bits a symbol.  No lag of an alphabet is
twice another or more, so that a period cannot be read as its own
multiple, and every lag is at most $T - 16$, so that the receiver sees
more than one period in a symbol.  The P20 lags are inside the range of
every codec's long-term predictor (AMR: 18 to 143 samples); the shortest
P10 lags are below AMR-WB's shortest (34 samples at 12.8~kHz, 2.66~ms)
and pass it nonetheless (Table~\ref{tab:pitch}): a signal of period $L$
has period $2L$ as well.  Lags above 70 samples were carried less surely
by every codec and are not used (Appendix~\ref{app:measurements}).

\subsubsection{Symbol timing}
A transmission in the parametric mode -- a message
(clause~\ref{sec:infomod}), a part of the probing signal L3
(Annex~\ref{annex:probe}) or the data of phase~4 -- shall begin with the
\emph{preamble}: eight symbols alternating the alphabet's lowest and
highest lag (index~0 and index~15), the lowest first.  The receiver
shall acquire the symbol timing from the preamble: the signal is
periodic within each symbol and not across the symbol boundaries, so the
symbol phase at which the normalized autocorrelation at the decided lag,
summed over the preamble's symbols, is greatest is the transmitter's.
The receiver should track the timing on the data symbols in the same
way, for the clock difference of clause~\ref{sec:pointp}~d), and shall
re-acquire it after a step of the received timing
(clause~\ref{sec:timing}); in P20 a step of one codec frame is one
symbol.

\subsubsection{Receiver}
The receiver shall decide each symbol's lag from the decoded samples
$y$ of the symbol: for each lag $L$ of the alphabet the normalized
autocorrelation
\[ c(L) = \frac{\sum_{m=0}^{T-L-1} y(m)\,y(m+L)}
   {\sqrt{\sum_{m=0}^{T-L-1} y(m)^2 \;\sum_{m=0}^{T-L-1} y(m+L)^2}}, \]
and the lag of the greatest $c(L)$; where a smaller lag's $c$ is within
0.02 of the greatest, the smaller lag should be taken (as the
measurements of Appendix~\ref{app:measurements} did).  The receiver
shall deliver, per symbol, the decided index's bits and a reliability
(the margin of the decided $c$ over the next), as clause~\ref{sec:mapping}
requires of a tone symbol.  Nothing of the codec's own pitch estimate is
used: the receiver has only the decoded samples.

\subsubsection{Raw rate}
Four bits a symbol: 200~bit/s with the 20~ms symbol, 400 with the 10~ms
symbol.  The probing chooses the symbol period, and chooses the
parametric mode only where no comb loads four tones
(clause~\ref{sec:loading}~e)): a comb of four DQPSK tones carries what
P10 does, and every comb that passes a codec mode carries more.

\itunote{The mode rests on one measured fact (Appendix~\ref{app:measurements},
Table~\ref{tab:pitch}): with the P20 alphabet, the period of the pulse
train came through every mode of AMR but 4.75~\kbs{}, every mode of
AMR-WB measured down to 6.6, EVS from 9.6~\kbs{} and two codecs in
tandem without a symbol in error in 198, while the combs carry nothing
through AMR 7.4, AMR-WB 6.6 or EVS 9.6 and V.21's FSK has a bit error
ratio of $4 \cdot 10^{-3}$ to $6 \cdot 10^{-3}$ through AMR 7.4 and up
to $3 \cdot 10^{-2}$ through EVS 9.6.  A codec at those modes spends
its bits on the spectral envelope and the pitch and codes the lag to the
sample (AMR: 8 or 9 bits in a frame's first subframe and 4 to 6 relative
to it in the others, at every mode); what it drops is the excitation's
detail, which is where a waveform's phases live.  With frame erasures
the mode's symbol error ratio is about twice the erasure ratio: the
erased frame, whose concealment repeats the previous lag, and often the
frame after it.}

\itunote{The two layers -- the comb's phases and the pulse train's
period -- were also measured as one waveform, with the comb's spacing as
the pitch: a symbol of one period of $L$ samples repeated, periodic at
$L$ so that the lag carries four bits, with a spectrum of harmonics of
$f_s/L$ whose phases carry the comb's bits as differences across
frequency, so that $L$ may change every symbol
(Appendix~\ref{app:measurements}, Table~\ref{tab:stack}).  It works and
is not worth having.  The lag's four bits cost the harmonics a reference
and come to 1529~bit/s against the comb's 1600 through AMR 12.2, AMR-WB
23.85 and EVS 24.4; where a comb is marginal the stacked signal is worse
(a tone symbol error ratio of 0.154 against 0.028 through AMR 10.2),
because the codec's phase error is more correlated from one symbol to
the next on a tone than from one tone to the next in a symbol, so that a
difference across frequency loses to \rec{V.22}'s difference in time;
and a harmonic, voiced-looking comb is not carried better than one with
random phases (both $10^{-3}$ at 200~Hz spacing through AMR 12.2; the
harmonic one worse through AMR 7.4).  The modes are therefore separate,
and the probing chooses.}

\subsection{Control sequences during data}\label{sec:control}
A DCE that wishes to renegotiate (clause~\ref{sec:reneg}) shall send, in
place of data, the control sequence, then its MP in the message mode
(clause~\ref{sec:infomod}).  In the comb modes the control sequence is
eight consecutive symbols with the phase difference $180^\circ$ on every
loaded tone; a receiver that decodes four such symbols in a row shall
stop delivering data to the inner decoder, flush, answer with the same
sequence if it has not sent one, and proceed to the MP exchange.  The
sequence cannot occur in scrambled data for more than two symbols with a
probability above $10^{-9}$ on 8 tones.  In the parametric mode the
control sequence is sixteen consecutive symbols at the alphabet's lowest
lag (index~0), and a receiver that decodes ten in a row acts as above
(ten equal symbols occur in scrambled data with a probability of
$2^{-36}$ at any position).

\subsection{Scrambling}
The bits given to the mapping of clause~\ref{sec:mapping} shall be
scrambled with the generating polynomials of \rec{V.34}: $1 + x^{-18} + x^{-23}$
in the calling DCE's transmitter and $1 + x^{-5} + x^{-23}$ in the
answering DCE's (GPC and GPA), self-synchronizing as in that
Recommendation.  The scrambler is reset at the start of phase~4 and after
each renegotiation.

\subsection{Quick start}\label{sec:quick}
A DCE may keep, for the last number dialled or answered, the loading and
the symbol period of the last call, and offer them in INFO0 as the mode
to start with.  If its peer accepts in its INFO0, phase~2 is reduced to L1 of
0.5~s for a check of the loading (or, where the loading kept is the
parametric mode, to the part of L3 for its symbol period), and the DCEs
proceed to phase~3; this
is in the manner of \rec{V.92}'s quick connect.  A loading so offered
shall still be checked by the receiver against the thresholds of
clause~\ref{sec:loading}.

\subsection{Loading with amplitude}\label{sec:qam}
\subsubsection{Signal}
A loaded tone may carry, besides its phase difference, a difference of
its amplitude from one symbol to the next: differential amplitude and
phase shift keying.  The tone's amplitude in symbol $n$ is one of $R$
rings, $R = 2$ or~4:
\[ a_{n,k} = g_R \, 10^{\rho_{n,k} \Delta_R / 20}, \qquad
   \rho_{n,k} = (\rho_{n-1,k} + d_{n,k}) \bmod R, \]
with the rings $\Delta_2 = 6$~dB apart for two rings and
$\Delta_4 = 3$~dB apart for four, the tone's amplitude digit
$d_{n,k} \in \{0, \ldots, R-1\}$, and $g_R$ the factor that gives the
tone the mean power of a tone without rings,
$g_R = \bigl(\frac{1}{R} \sum_{r=0}^{R-1} 10^{r \Delta_R / 10}\bigr)^{-1/2}$.
The transmitted signal of clause~\ref{sec:comb} is then
\[ s_n(t) = A \sum_{k \in \mathcal{L}} a_{n,k} \cos\bigl(2\pi f_k t + \varphi_{n,k}\bigr), \]
with $a_{n,k} = 1$ on tones without rings.  The phase reference symbol
(clause~\ref{sec:modulation}) has $\rho = 0$ on every tone.  A tone with
rings carries its phase bits (Table~\ref{tab:gray}) and then its
amplitude bits: one for two rings ($d$ the bit), two for four, Gray coded
(00 is $d = 0$, 01 is 1, 11 is 2, 10 is 3).

\subsubsection{Receiver}
The receiver decides $d$ from the ratio of the tone's magnitudes in two
consecutive symbols, as it decides the phase from their phase
difference, with no amplitude reference:
\[ \hat d_{n,k} = \operatorname{round}\Bigl(\frac{20 \log_{10} \bigl(|r_{n,k}| / |r_{n-1,k}|\bigr)}{\Delta_R}\Bigr) \bmod R, \]
where $r_{n,k}$ is the tone's integral over the useful part; the
reliability of clause~\ref{sec:mapping} is the distance of the ratio
(in dB) from the nearest decision boundary.

\subsubsection{Loading}
The loading of clause~\ref{sec:loading} gives a tone two rings where the
rms change $\sigma_A$ of its magnitude from one symbol of L1 to the next
(whose amplitude is constant) is at most 0.8~dB, and four rings where it
is at most 0.4~dB, on the phase order that clause chose for the tone; it
does so only where both DCEs indicated the capability in CM and JM
(Annex~\ref{annex:v8}) and the mode set allows it.  The raw rate's $b_k$
(clause~\ref{sec:modulation}) is then the tone's phase bits plus one for
two rings or two for four: 4 for 8-DPSK with two rings, 5 with four.  A
DCE that does not receive its peer's MP with the rings it asked for
loads without them.

\itunote{The parameters come from the measurements of
Appendix~\ref{app:evs} (Table~\ref{tab:dapsk}).  Of 725 tones measured
through EVS (24.4 to 128~\kbs{}), AMR and AMR-WB, two rings 6~dB apart
held (a tone symbol error ratio below $2 \cdot 10^{-2}$ for the phase and
for the rings) on 99 per cent of those whose $\sigma_A$ was at most
0.8~dB and on 17 per cent of the others; four rings 3~dB apart held on
all of those at or below 0.4~dB and on fewer than half of those up to
0.8.  A tone's $\sigma_A$ is therefore the loading's one criterion, as
its phase error is for the phase order, and it finds the tones without
a list of codecs: through EVS at 96 and 128~\kbs{} the rings add 21 to
67 per cent to the comb's raw rate (15\,100~bit/s on S200 at
128~\kbs{}, against 10\,350 with phase alone), through EVS at 48~\kbs{}
wideband and 24.4~\kbs{} narrowband 19 to 51 per cent, through AMR-WB at
23.85 and AMR 12.2 a few tones of the 300 and 400~Hz combs, and
through AMR 10.2 none.  A coherent
constellation (QAM with a trellis code) was not taken: it needs the
reference and the equalization the comb's differential modulation does
without, and the codec's phase error, correlated from one symbol to the
next, is what differential detection cancels.}
