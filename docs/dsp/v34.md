# V.8 and V.34

**V.8** is the short exchange at the start of a modern modem call. In this exchange,
the two modems tell which standards they support, and they agree on one.
**V.34** is the fastest standard for an ordinary analogue phone line, up
to 33600 bit/s. Also, V.90 and V.92 use it as their base.

The code is `dsp/dp_v34.cpp` and the `dsp/v34_*.cpp` files. For each
step of the start-up, the signals and the fixes for other modems,
refer to [the technical details](v34-details.md).

## V.8: agreeing on a standard

When the answering modem answers the call, it sends its answer tone. Then
these steps occur:

1. The calling modem replies with a list of its functions.
2. The answering modem replies with the functions that the two modems share.
3. The call continues with the best of those functions.

Some older modems do not support V.8. With such a modem, D-Modem
tries V.32bis, V.22bis, V.21, V.23 and Bell 103, in that sequence,
until one connects.

## V.34: how it gets its speed

Before V.34 modems send data, they measure the line for some seconds:

1. They send test tones. These tones show how much of the frequency range the
   line carries, and how much noise the line has.
2. They select a symbol rate (the number of signal changes in one second) and a
   carrier frequency that are applicable to the line.
3. They train their equalisers and echo cancellers. The equalisers are filters
   that cancel the distortion of the line. The echo cancellers remove the echo of
   the signal of the modem itself.
4. They agree on a data rate, from 2400 to 33600 bit/s.

If the line becomes worse during the call, the modems **retrain** (they measure
again) or **renegotiate** to a different speed.

## How well it works

Between two D-Modems on a simulated line, V.34 gets its full 33600
bit/s on a clean line. When the noise increases, the speed decreases
(for example, 28800 bit/s at a 35 dB signal-to-noise ratio, 19200 at 25 dB).
V.34 also connects with the modem of each other company in the test suite
([Interop](../interop.md)).

## Half-duplex V.34 for fax

Fax machines can also use V.34 (T.30 Annex F), in a half-duplex form.
One side sends the pages at up to 33600 bit/s. Between pages, a slow 1200 bit/s
control channel carries the fax messages in the two directions.
D-Modem has this modem. We tested it with two of these modems back to back. Refer to
[the technical details](v34-details.md#half-duplex-clause-12-for-fax).
