# V.61: talking and sending data on one carrier (ASVD)

With **V.61** (ITU-T V.61, "ASVD", analogue simultaneous voice and data),
two modems can carry a telephone conversation and a data connection at the
same time on one call. The ITU approved it in 1996, together with the
DSVD of V.70, but it operates in a very different method:

- DSVD compresses the voice and sends it as data.
- ASVD sends the voice as an analogue signal. It adds this signal directly
  onto the carrier of the modem, around each data point.

The data goes at 4800 bit/s while a person speaks. When the two persons are quiet, the modems
change to data alone at up to 14400 bit/s. V.61 came from the
VoiceSpan modems of AT&T.

D-Modem has its own V.61 data pump, written from the
Recommendation. Two D-Modems agree on ASVD in V.8bis, do the start-up of V.61,
and connect. The data of the computer goes over V.42, as on all
calls. The speech comes from the same source as for V.70: the handset of a SmartUSB56,
or a program of your own with the `--dsvd-audio` option of dmodemd.

For the full technical description, refer to
[the technical details](v61-details.md).

## Turning it on

The two ends must have V.8bis and ASVD enabled:

    AT+A8E=1,1,,1+ASVD=1

`+ASVD` is a command of D-Modem. With `1`, the modem offers ASVD in V.8bis, and it selects
ASVD when the other end also offers it. With `0` (the default), it does not.
`AT+ASVD?` shows the setting. If only one end has ASVD, the call is an
ordinary data call.

If the two ends also offer the DSVD of V.70 (`+DSVD=1`), the modems select DSVD,
because DSVD carries more data together with the voice on a good line.

`AT+MS=V61` (or `AT+MS=61`) starts V.61 immediately, without V.8bis.
The two ends must have this setting, because no other modem standard falls back to V.61.

## What the user sees

`ATD` and `ATA` operate as usual. When the two
ends have a voice path, `CONNECT` reports 4800 bit/s, because the call can always carry that rate. While
the line is quiet, the data goes faster than that. If neither end has a voice path,
`CONNECT` reports the full speed of data alone.

## Where the voice goes

As for [V.70](../v70.md#where-the-voice-goes):

- **A SmartUSB56 with a handset** (`--usb-voice-codec`): the phone on
  the handset jack of the modem.
- **`--dsvd-audio <command>`** (dmodemd, not on Windows): a program that
  plays the speech of the far end and records our speech, as 16 bit samples at 8000 each
  second.

If neither item is available, the modem tells the other end that it has no voice. Then each
frame carries only data.

## Speeds in simulation

Two D-Modems back to back on a simulated phone line (`pump_test`,
`dsp_loopback`), without speech:

| Line's signal to noise | Speed |
|---|---|
| 35 dB and more | 14400 bit/s |
| 24 to 30 dB | 12000 bit/s |
| 20 to 22 dB | 9600 bit/s |
| 18 dB | 7200 bit/s |
| 15 dB and less | 4800 bit/s |

With a tone on the microphone of each side, the data goes at 4800 bit/s
without a bit error down to 20 dB. Each side hears more than 90% of
the tone of the other side. All of these results are true in these conditions:

- At the two symbol rates of V.61 (3000 and 2800 each second).
- At 8000 and 9600 samples each second.
- With clock offsets of 300 ppm.
- With near and far echoes.

## Limits

- We tried it only between two D-Modems. We did not try a V.61 modem from a different
  manufacturer.
- We did not try the voice on the handset of a real SmartUSB56 yet. We tried it
  only in tests.
- The audio pre-emphasis filter has a fixed flat setting. V.61 lets
  a modem select a different filter for each frame, to suit the speech.
- The modem does not do the rate renegotiation of clause 11. If the line of a call becomes
  worse, the call retrains instead.
- The modem does not use the auxiliary control channel (up to 342 bit/s in the control
  segments).
- The modems agree on ASVD only at the start of a call, not during a
  telephone conversation.
