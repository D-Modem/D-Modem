# V.92

V.92 is the last of the dial-up modem standards (2000). It keeps V.90's
56000 bit/s download and adds three things:

- **Quick connect.** The modem remembers the line from the last call,
  so the next call's start-up takes a few seconds instead of twenty or
  more.
- **Modem-on-hold.** When call waiting rings during a modem call, the
  modem can put the data call on hold while you answer the phone, then
  carry on.
- **PCM upstream.** The upload direction can also use the digital
  method, raising it from 33600 to up to 48000 bit/s.

This page is about D-Modem as the **digital** end of a V.92 call (the
internet provider's side). For the analogue end, see
[V.90 and V.92: the analogue modem](v90a.md). For every signal and
setting, see [the technical details](v92-details.md).

## What D-Modem does

As the digital modem, with `AT+MS=92` (the default) on a digital line,
D-Modem supports all three features:

- It answers a calling modem's quick connect request and skips the long
  start-up.
- It holds the call when the analogue modem asks, for as long as the
  modem asks, and picks it up again after.
- It receives PCM upstream, and can change the upload speed during the
  call as the line changes.

In the tests the Conexant HSF modem reaches 52000 bit/s down and 41333
up with PCM upstream, and the ESS modem 49333 down and 41333 up (see
[Interop](../interop.md)).

## Settings

- `DSP_V92=0` turns V.92 off (the call is V.90 instead).
- `DSP_V92_QC=0` turns quick connect off.
- `DSP_V92_PCMUP=0` turns PCM upstream off.
