# V.92

V.92 is the last of the dial-up modem standards (2000). It keeps the
56000 bit/s download of V.90 and adds three functions:

- **Quick connect.** A shorter start-up. The analogue modem can skip
  parts of V.8 and the line probing. Thus, the call connects faster.
- **Modem-on-hold.** If call waiting rings during a modem call, the
  modem can put the data call on hold. You can answer the telephone, and
  then the data call continues.
- **PCM upstream.** The upload direction can also use the digital
  method. This increases its speed from 33600 to a maximum of 48000 bit/s.

This page is about D-Modem as the **digital** end of a V.92 call (the
side of the internet provider). For the analogue end, refer to
[V.90 and V.92: the analogue modem](v90a.md). For all signals and
settings, refer to [the technical details](v92-details.md).

## What D-Modem does

As the digital modem, with `AT+MS=92` (the default) on a digital line,
D-Modem supports the three functions:

- It answers the quick connect request of a modem that calls, and skips
  the long start-up.
- It holds the call when the analogue modem asks, for the time that the
  modem asks. Then it continues the call.
- It receives PCM upstream. It can change the upload speed during the
  call if the line changes.

In the tests, the Conexant HSF modem gets 52000 bit/s down and 41333
up with PCM upstream. The ESS modem gets 49333 down and 41333 up (refer
to [Interop](../interop.md)).

## Settings

- `DSP_V92=0` disables V.92 (the call is V.90).
- `DSP_V92_QC=0` disables quick connect.
- `DSP_V92_PCMUP=0` disables PCM upstream.
