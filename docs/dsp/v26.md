# V.26 ter and V.26 bis: 2400 bit/s on a dial-up line

**V.26 ter** is the 2400 bit/s modem of the ITU for ordinary phone lines,
from 1984. The two ends send at the same time on the same pair of wires,
each on the same 1800 Hz carrier. Each modem removes its own echo
from the signal that it receives (echo cancelling), as V.32 does later at higher
speeds. On a poor line, it decreases its speed to 1200 bit/s. Its use was mostly
in France and in Europe. In other countries, V.22bis did the same work.

D-Modem has V.26 ter from the text of the Recommendation. Two D-Modems
connect with it at 2400 or 1200 bit/s. The interop tests of D-Modem have no V.26 ter
modems. Thus, we did not try it against a modem from a different manufacturer.

Its older relatives:

- **V.26** (and Bell 201, its "alternative B") are 2400 bit/s modems
  for 4-wire leased lines. D-Modem has them (`AT&L2+MS=V26`, refer to
  [leased lines](../leased.md)).
- **V.26 bis** is the dial-up version from 1972, before echo
  cancelling. D-Modem also has it: refer to [V.26 bis](#v26-bis) below.

For the full technical description, refer to
[the technical details](v26-details.md).

## Using it

    AT+MS=V26T       (or AT+MS=126, or AT+MS=V26TER)

The two ends must have this setting. V.26 ter starts with an answer tone and its own
handshake, not with V.8. Thus, a modem that does not have this setting does not find it.
`AT+MS=V26T,0,1200,1200` keeps the call at 1200 bit/s.

The start-up takes about 7 seconds after the answer tone:

1. The answering modem tells which speeds it can use.
2. The calling modem selects one and tells it.
3. Each modem in turn sends for one second, so that its echo canceller
   can learn its own echo.
4. The two modems send at the same time, and the data starts.

V.26 ter has no retrain. If the line becomes too bad, the call stops.

## In simulation

Two D-Modems back to back on a simulated phone line (`pump_test` and
`dmodem_test`) connect at 2400 bit/s:

| Line | Result |
|---|---|
| clean | 2400 bit/s, no errors |
| 15 dB signal to noise | 2400 bit/s, no errors |
| each modem's own echo 6 dB below its signal | 2400 bit/s, no errors (the canceller takes away 28 dB) |
| 8000 Hz sampling, a 4x level change, a 300 ppm clock difference | 2400 bit/s, no errors |
| `AT+MS=V26T,0,1200,1200` | 1200 bit/s |

## V.26 bis

**V.26 bis** is the older dial-up 2400 bit/s modem. It has no echo
canceller. Thus, only one end at a time can send at 2400 bit/s. The other
end can send back slowly, at 75 bit/s, on a separate low tone (the
"backward channel"), as V.23 does at 1200/75. On a poor line, it decreases its speed to 1200
bit/s.

V.26 bis lets the fast direction change (half duplex). D-Modem
does not do this. As with its V.23, the answering modem always sends at 2400 (or
1200) bit/s, and the calling modem always sends at 75 bit/s. This is applicable when
a host or BBS sends screens to a terminal, and a person types on the terminal.

    AT+MS=V26B       (or AT+MS=226, or AT+MS=V26BIS)

The two ends must have this setting. The `+MS` of the answering modem sets the speed.
`AT+MS=V26B,0,1200,1200` on the answering modem sets 1200 bit/s, and the calling
modem uses the same speed, independently of its own `+MS`. V.26 bis has no method to
agree on a speed. The caller finds the speed from the first signal of the answerer.
V.42 error correction is disabled, as on V.23.

The start-up takes about 1.2 seconds after the answer tone. The
answering modem sends its synchronizing signal for one second. Then the
calling modem starts its 75 bit/s tone, and the two modems show CONNECT.

In simulation (`pump_test`, `dmodem_test`), two D-Modems connect at
2400/75 bit/s with no errors in these conditions:

- On a clean line.
- At 15 dB of signal to noise.
- At 8000 Hz, with a 4x level change and a 300 ppm clock
  difference.
- With the echo of each modem 6 dB below its signal.

`AT+MS=V26B,0,1200,1200` connects at 1200/75. The interop tests of D-Modem have no V.26 bis
modems.
