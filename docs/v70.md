# V.70: voice and data on one call (DSVD)

With **V.70** (ITU-T V.70, "DSVD", digital simultaneous voice and data),
two modems send a telephone conversation and a data connection at the
same time on one call. The modem compresses the voice (G.729 Annex A,
8 kbit/s) and sends it as data, together with the data of the computer,
on one V.34 (or faster) connection. In the middle of the 1990s, companies
sold it as "talk while you play" for modem games and remote help.

It has these parts:

- **V.8bis**. In V.8bis, the two modems agree to use DSVD at the start of
  the call ([V.8bis](dsp/v8bis.md)).
- **V.76**, a multiplexer that shares the connection between some
  channels (one for data, one for voice).
- **V.75**, which opens and closes those channels.
- G.729 Annex A, the voice coder.

D-Modem has the four parts. Two D-Modems agree on DSVD and connect. Then
the data of the computer goes through the data channel of V.76. If the
modem has a voice path (refer to the subsequent sections), a voice
channel opens next to the data channel. The speech of each side is coded
with G.729 Annex A, 10 octets each 10 ms, and sent before the data.

D-Modem has its own G.729 Annex A coder. On all of the Annex A test
vectors of the Recommendation, it gives the same bits and the same
samples as the ITU-T reference. We also tried it in the two directions
with bcg729, which is the coder that most open source telephone software
uses.

When nobody speaks, the coder also stops (G.729 Annex B). When the
background noise changes, it sends a short description of the noise. It
sends nothing between these descriptions. Thus, the data gets that part
of the line. The far end plays a matching "comfort noise". Thus, the line
always has a sound. On all of the test vectors of Annex B, this silence
coder also gives the bits and samples of the ITU-T reference.

For the full technical description, refer to
[the technical details](v70-details.md).

## Turning it on

Enable V.8bis and DSVD on the two ends:

    AT+A8E=1,1,,1+DSVD=1

`+DSVD` is a command of D-Modem only. `1` offers DSVD in V.8bis, and
selects it when the other end also offers it. `0` (the default) does not
offer it. `AT+DSVD?` shows the setting.

If DSVD is enabled at only one end, the call continues as a plain data
call with V.42 error correction, as without DSVD.

## What the user sees

`ATD` and `ATA` operate as usual. `CONNECT` comes when the data channel
is open. Then the data of the computer moves as on all calls with error
correction. Where the modem gives the error correction of the call
(`ATI11`, the `Protocol:` report), it shows `V.76`. The data channel has
no compression.

## Where the voice goes

The voice channel must have a location to send and play speech:

- **A SmartUSB56 with a handset** (`--usb-voice-codec`): the telephone on
  the handset jack of the modem. While the voice channel is open, the
  modem sends its microphone to the far end. It plays the speech of the
  far end in its earpiece.
- **`--dsvd-audio <command>`** (dmodemd, not on Windows): a program that
  starts when the voice channel opens and stops when it closes. It gets
  the speech of the far end on its input. It gives our speech on its
  output. The format is raw 16 bit little-endian samples, 8000 each
  second, mono. For example, with the ALSA tools:

      dmodemd ... --dsvd-audio 'aplay -q -t raw -f S16_LE -r 8000 & exec arecord -q -t raw -f S16_LE -r 8000'

If neither is available, the call is data only, as before. The modem
refuses a voice channel that the other end asks for.

[V.61](dsp/v61.md) (`+ASVD`), the analogue type of voice and data, uses
the same voice path.

## Limits

- We tried the voice only in tests, not on the handset of a SmartUSB56.
- When the line is slow, the voice gets 8 kbit/s of it first. The data
  gets the remaining part.
- It operates only between two D-Modems now. We did not try it with the
  DSVD modem of a different manufacturer.
- DSVD operates on the modulation that V.8 selects after it. V.70 gives
  V.34 and V.32bis. But two D-Modems on a digital line select V.91 (up to
  62667 bps).
- The modems agree on DSVD only at the start of a call. They cannot
  agree on it during a telephone conversation.
- There is one data channel, in error-recovery mode, with the default
  settings of V.76, and one voice channel. The modem refuses a channel
  with other settings that the other end asks for. It also refuses a
  second channel.
- The modem uses silence suppression only when the two ends ask for the
  audio header of V.75. Two D-Modems do this. A DSVD modem of a different
  manufacturer can accept the header but have no Annex B decoder. Such a
  modem hears gaps, not comfort noise. We did not try such a modem.
