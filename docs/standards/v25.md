# V.25: answer tones and echo control

This page describes ITU-T Recommendation V.25 (10/1996), "Automatic
answering equipment and general procedures for automatic calling
equipment on the general switched telephone network including procedures
for disabling of echo control devices for both manually and automatically
established calls". It is our own description. It is not a copy of the
Recommendation. For the normative text, refer to the Recommendation
(`docs/specs/fetch.sh V.25`). For the timing of each signal, refer to
[the technical details](v25-details.md).

## Summary

V.25 tells how an automatic modem answers a call, and how the modems
disable the echo control equipment of the telephone network. Most of the
older modulations (V.21, V.22, V.22bis, V.32 and others) start with the
procedure of V.25.

V.25 has three signals:

- **The calling tone (CT).** The call modem sends short bursts of
  1300 Hz. The tone tells a person who answers that a machine calls.
- **The answer tone (ANS).** The answer modem sends 2100 Hz for about
  3 seconds. The tone tells the call modem that a modem answered.
- **Phase reversals.** The answer modem can reverse the phase of the
  answer tone each 450 ms. The phase reversals disable the echo
  cancellers of the network.

## Why the network must not control echo

A long telephone circuit has echo suppressors or echo cancellers. They
remove the echo of speech. But a duplex modem sends and receives at the
same time. An echo suppressor can stop one direction, and an echo
canceller can change the signal. A 2100 Hz tone disables the echo
suppressors. A 2100 Hz tone with phase reversals also disables the echo
cancellers. The equipment stays disabled while the line has a signal. If
the line is silent for more than about 100 ms, the equipment can start
again.

## How an automatic call starts

1. The call modem dials. Then it can send the calling tone.
2. The answer modem connects to the line. It is silent for about 2
   seconds.
3. The answer modem sends the answer tone, with or without phase
   reversals.
4. The call modem identifies the answer tone. It stops the calling tone.
5. The answer tone stops. Each modem waits for 75 ms.
6. The modems start their modulation.

A person can also start a data call by hand. For example, a person can
push the data button of a modem when the person hears the answer tone.

## Related standards

- [V.8](v8.md): ANSam, the answer tone of modern modems. It adds an
  amplitude modulation to the 2100 Hz tone of V.25.
- V.25 bis and V.25 ter (now V.250): the commands that tell a modem to
  dial.
- G.164 and G.165: the echo suppressors and the echo cancellers of the
  network.

## V.25 in D-Modem

`dsp/dp_callprog.cpp` dials, sends the calling tone and identifies the
answer tone. The pumps of each modulation send the answer tone when
D-Modem answers. `dsp/dp_v34.cpp` sends ANSam with phase reversals for
V.8.
