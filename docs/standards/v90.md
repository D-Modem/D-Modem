# V.90: 56000 bit/s downstream, with a digital modem and an analogue modem

This page describes ITU-T Recommendation V.90 (09/98), "A digital modem
and analogue modem pair for use on the Public Switched Telephone Network
(PSTN) at data signalling rates of up to 56 000 bit/s downstream and up
to 33 600 bit/s upstream". It is our own description. It is not a copy of
the Recommendation. For the normative text, refer to the Recommendation
(`docs/specs/fetch.sh V.90`). For the signals, the start-up and the
timing, refer to [the technical details](v90-details.md).

## Summary

A V.90 call has two different modems:

- The **digital modem** is on a digital connection to the telephone
  network. It sends the PCM codes of G.711 directly, 8000 codes each
  second.
- The **analogue modem** is on an analogue telephone line. The codec of
  the telephone exchange changes each code into a voltage on that line.
  The analogue modem measures these voltages and finds the codes.

Thus, the direction from the digital modem to the analogue modem
(downstream) can send 28000 bit/s to 56000 bit/s. The other direction
(upstream) is V.34, at 4800 bit/s to 33600 bit/s.

- Each data frame has six symbols. The digital modem selects each code
  from a set of codes (a constellation) that the analogue modem can tell
  apart.
- A modulus encoder puts the data bits on the six symbols. Some of the
  sign bits can shape the spectrum of the signal.
- The start-up has four phases:
  1. V.8.
  2. The line probe and the round-trip delay, as in V.34.
  3. The training of the equalizers and the digital impairment learning
     (DIL). The analogue modem finds what the network does to each code.
  4. The selection of the constellations and the rates.
- A rate renegotiation changes the rates without a full retrain.

## Where V.90 is used

In the 1990s, the equipment of an internet provider was the digital
modem, and the modem of the user was the analogue modem. If the call
does not have a digital modem at one end, or the network changes the
codes too much, the modems use V.34.

## Related standards

- [V.8](v8.md): the exchange that selects V.90.
- [V.34](v34.md): phase 2, the upstream direction and the fallback.
- [V.91](v91.md): PCM in the two directions, between two digital modems.
- [V.92](v92.md): the next standard. It adds quick connect, modem-on-hold and PCM
  upstream.
- G.711: the µ-law and A-law PCM codes.
