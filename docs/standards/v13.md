# V.13: simulated carrier control

This page describes ITU-T Recommendation V.13 (03/1993), "Simulated
carrier control". It is our own description. It is not a copy of the
Recommendation. For the normative text, refer to the Recommendation
(`docs/specs/fetch.sh V.13`). For the patterns and the counts, refer to
[the technical details](v13-details.md).

## Summary

Some old terminals send data in one direction at a time. Such a terminal
sets circuit 105 (request to send) ON when it starts to send. The modem
then turns its carrier ON. At the far end, the modem sees the carrier and
sets circuit 109 (carrier detected) ON. Thus, the far terminal knows that
data comes.

Modern modems cannot turn the carrier OFF and ON quickly. Their training
is too long. V.13 gives a different method. The carrier stays ON all the
time. In place of the carrier, the modem sends special bit patterns in
the data:

- **The idle pattern.** While circuit 105 is OFF, the modem sends
  scrambled ONE bits. The far modem identifies this pattern and sets
  circuit 109 OFF.
- **The ON pattern.** When circuit 105 comes ON, the modem sends 8
  scrambled ZERO bits. Then it sends the data. The far modem identifies
  this pattern and sets circuit 109 ON.

## Where V.13 is used

V.13 applies when a carrier cannot go OFF and ON. Examples are:

- The sub-channels of a multiplexer.
- Modems that have long training sequences for the equalizer or the echo
  canceller.
- A PCM channel that carries 64 kbit/s data.

## Related standards

- V.24: the definitions of circuits 105, 106 and 109.
- [V.54](https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-V.54): the loop tests. V.13 does
  not specify where the function is with respect to the loops.

## V.13 in D-Modem

`core/v13.cpp` and `core/v13.h` implement V.13 between the data link
layer and the data pump. To enable it, set `S48=1`. A pty has no circuit
105. Thus, D-Modem sets circuit 105 ON when data comes from the DTE, and
OFF when the DTE stops.
