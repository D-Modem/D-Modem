# G.711: PCM of voice frequencies

This page describes ITU-T Recommendation G.711 (11/88), "Pulse code
modulation (PCM) of voice frequencies". It is our own description. It
is not a copy of the Recommendation. For the normative text, refer to
the Recommendation (`docs/specs/fetch.sh G.711`). For the codes and the
values, refer to [the technical details](g711-details.md).

## Summary

G.711 codes each sample of a voice signal in 8 bits. The digital
telephone network uses it at 8000 samples each second (64 kbit/s).

- There are two laws. Europe and most countries use the A-law. North
  America and Japan use the µ-law.
- Each law is logarithmic: small signals get small steps, and large
  signals get large steps. Each law has 8 segments of 16 steps for each
  polarity.
- Bit 1 of the octet is the polarity. Bits 2 to 4 are the segment. Bits
  5 to 8 are the step in the segment.
- The A-law inverts the even bits on the line. The µ-law inverts all the
  bits.
- Tables convert µ-law octets to A-law octets and back. The digital path
  between two countries with different laws uses the A-law.

## Where G.711 is used

All digital telephone exchanges, ISDN and most voice over IP calls use
G.711. A V.90, V.91 or V.92 digital modem sends G.711 octets directly.

## Related standards

- [V.90](v90.md), [V.91](v91.md) and [V.92](v92.md): the modems that use
  the G.711 octets.
- G.191: the software tools of ITU-T, with a reference G.711 coder.
- G.729: a coder at 8 kbit/s, for comparison.

## G.711 in D-Modem

`dsp/g711.h` has the encoders and the decoders of the two laws. The PCM
modems, the clear channel and the test networks use it.
