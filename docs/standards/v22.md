# V.22: 1200 bit/s duplex DPSK

This page describes ITU-T Recommendation V.22 (11/1988), "1200 bits per
second duplex modem standardized for use in the general switched
telephone network and on point-to-point 2-wire leased telephone-type
circuits". It is our own description. It is not a copy of the
Recommendation. For the normative text, refer to the Recommendation
(`docs/specs/fetch.sh V.22`). For the signal, the handshake and the
timing, refer to [the technical details](v22-details.md).

## Summary

V.22 sends data at 1200 bit/s (or 600 bit/s), in the two directions at
the same time, on a usual telephone line. It was the usual modem of the
early 1980s, together with Bell 212A in North America.

V.22 divides the line into two channels, as V.21 does:

- **The low channel**, with a carrier at 1200 Hz. The call modem sends
  on it.
- **The high channel**, with a carrier at 2400 Hz. The answer modem sends
  on it.

Each channel uses differential phase shift keying (DPSK) at 600 baud.
Each symbol is a change of the phase of the carrier: 0°, 90°, 180° or
270°. Thus, each symbol carries two bits (a dibit). A scrambler makes
the data random before the modulation. Thus, the receiver can keep its
timing on all types of data.

## How a V.22 call starts

1. The answer modem sends the answer tone of [V.25](v25.md).
2. The answer modem sends unscrambled ONE bits in the high channel.
3. The call modem detects them. It waits 456 ms, then sends scrambled ONE
   bits in the low channel.
4. The answer modem detects the scrambled ONE bits. It sends scrambled
   ONE bits too.
5. After a fixed time (765 ms), the two modems start to send data.

V.22 has no adaptive equalizer and no retrain. A fixed equalizer is
enough at 600 baud.

## Variants

V.22 has three alternatives (A, B and C). They are different in their
data modes: synchronous only, or also asynchronous (start-stop)
characters. Alternative C also has a special mode that sends slow
asynchronous data (up to 300 bit/s) without a buffer.

## Related standards

- [V.22 bis](v22bis.md): 2400 bit/s on the same channels. A V.22 bis
  modem also operates as a V.22 modem.
- [V.25](v25.md): the answer tone.
- V.14: the change from start-stop characters to synchronous bits.
- [V.54](https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-V.54): the test loops.
- Bell 212A: the North American 1200 bit/s modem. Its modulation is
  almost the same, but its start-up is different.
